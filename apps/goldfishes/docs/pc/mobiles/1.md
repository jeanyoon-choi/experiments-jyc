The second of NOTES 1023's three screens, Goldfishes Mobiles. The concept is the mobile ready-made: Duchamp, Warhol and Koons applied to software.

- **Platforms:** the platforms themselves, as hyper-real clones (NOTES 1023, 웹 레디메이드 ②), not images of them.
- **Phones:** each phone follows one goldfish of the main screen. When its goldfish reaches a bubble, the phone shows that bubble's keyword as the newest post in one of eight apps.
- **Apps:** the seven deadly sins' platforms of NOTES 1017 plus the news. The same hype event reaches all eight, each in its own grammar: the UI is only a wrapper, and the real thing is the goldfish's attention and the hype's propagation.

## Phone (`phone/`), as left on 2026-10-28

The frame is drawn at native pt (404 × 858, glass 390 × 844) and shrunk with CSS `zoom`, not `transform: scale`, so its layers rasterize at the size shown (with scale, 32 phones ran at 10 fps). `--glass-*` stands in for the viewport.

The last pass followed one rule: nothing appears or disappears inside the glass; everything enters by motion.

- **Feed.** A conveyor (`useStream`). Every phones ÷ `updatesPerSec` it puts one post on below the view: the newest goldfish item, or a repost of a recent one. It takes the oldest off only once that post is wholly above the view (`scroller.ts` `above`). The scroller keeps the post in view as an anchor, so content changing above it does not move the screen. It cruises and flicks without stopping. Clones get posts oldest first.
- **Tinder and Netflix.** The next card is mounted beneath, and the shown one is swiped off it (aside, or up).
- **App changes.** Each phone has its own clock, about once per phones ÷ `hopsPerSec`. Under "mix" it does one of three things:
  - taps a banner from the next app, which opens out of the banner's rect (clip-path);
  - swipes along the home bar;
  - opens the app switcher.

  The app left behind stays opaque beneath.
- **Banners.** iOS notifications, one per phones ÷ `bannersPerSec`, from the newest item's app (`appOfItem`).
- **First item.** The lock screen slides up off the app.
- **Images.** Images are eager with `decoding="sync"`. All portraits are decoded up front (`preloadPortraits`).

This pass was not watched in motion before the verdict.

## Option menu (`controls/`, `model/tuning.ts`)

Round triggers at the bottom right, in bubble/7's style (its CSS copied). Every parameter can also start from the query (`?hop=0&cardMs=600`).

- **앱 이동:** transition style, share of changes made through a notification, changes a second over the grid, transition length.
- **움직임:** phone count, posts a second over the grid, flick speed, pause, cruise speed, banners a second, banner hold.

The rate items arrive at is the main screen's (`slots.mjs` `dwellMs`).

## Apps (`apps/`)

The contract is `apps/types.ts`: a pure `Screen({posts})` (newest first), `Icon`, `statusTone` and `notify`. `apps/registry.ts` lists the eight apps. A phone takes them by slot (`appForSlot`): every app once in each run of eight, the order turned by three each run, so the grid mixes apps rather than stacking one per column. A later pc version may swap the set.

| App | Sin | Grammar of the event |
|---|---|---|
| Tinder | Lust | a profile card whose bio is the hype |
| Yelp | Gluttony | the hype as a venue under review |
| LinkedIn | Greed | "I'm thrilled to announce…", document carousel |
| Netflix | Sloth | the hype as a docuseries billboard and Top 10 |
| X | Wrath | rants, ratios, quote-posts |
| Facebook | Envy | shares with a link card |

| Apple News | the eighth axis | Top Stories |

## Content (`content/`)

- `events.ts`: two hype events per keyword. Hyper-real yet parodic: the facts are plausible fictions with fictional orgs, never real reporting. A keyword with no events gets a generic event from its label, so a screen's new keyword never breaks a phone.
- `keywords.ts`: the demo vocabulary, matching bubble/7. A live screen's vocabulary wins.
- `images.ts`: every image URL in one place. `imageFor` is currently empty, so clones draw stand-in graphics. `portraitFor` uses the local `tech-power-faces`.
  - Open question: those are real people. See Open below.
- `posts.ts`: a slot's history becomes posts.

## Performance

- **CSS zoom against scale.** With `transform: scale`, 32 phones ran at 10 fps. With CSS `zoom` they ran at 52 fps.
- **Earlier fps readings are void.** The 59–60 fps measured on 2026-10-27 were taken while no feed scrolled.
  - `scroller.ts` cached the last written offset as `NaN` and wrote only when `Math.abs(y − shown) >= 0.25`.
  - Any comparison with `NaN` is false, so no transform was ever written.
  - The bug was fixed on 2026-10-28.
- **First reading with scrolling (2026-10-28).** All 43 visible feeds scrolled in the 380-fish demo at 64 phones. The page ran at 51.8 fps, with a p95 frame of 33 ms.
- **Before the fix, same build.** The page ran at 58.4 fps with about 12 app changes a second. Feeds were static then.

## Failure record and handoff (2026-10-28)

### What the user wanted

They wanted a visual spectacle of the phone-farming, agentic era: a field of many phones, each reacting to the goldfish of the main screen (`screen/bubble/7`) as they strike bubbles. In the user's words:

- "연속적으로 바뀌고 끊임없이 정보가 유통되는 화려한 모바일의 스펙타클"
- "hyper real 한 phone transition notification 등이 빠른 스피드로 나타나는 자본주의적 폰 경제의 스펙타클"
- "빠르게 전환되는 시각적 스펙타클, 여러 모바일에서 하는, 버블에 반응하는"

Conditions they stated along the way:

- The feel of SCC [`/mobiles/1`](../../../../scc/docs/experiments/mobiles/README.md) (`https://<host>:2000/mobiles/1`): every screen change animates, and in-app motion plays on a timeline. See "애니메이션도 mobiles/1 에 있었던 것처럼".
- Continuous and smooth. There must never be a blink, flash, jump or step ("깜빡거림, 불연속, 플래시가 있으면 아예 안됨"). They asked for a structural fix, not one patch per symptom.
- Real app-to-app transitions are what counts, not the number of notifications ("알림 숫자가 문제가 아니라 실질적인 전환").
- All 380 fish feed the phones. No phone is ever dead or dark, and the feed updates within seconds.
- Performance must never be the bottleneck.
- Parameters live client-side, so tuning needs no relay restart.
- Judge by watching the motion over time, not by single frames ("시간에 따른 움직임을 쭉 보는 것이 좋아").

The user's verdict on what was built: "느린 구닥다리 같은 풍경". It looked discontinuous, laggy and dull, and nothing in it read as a rapidly switching spectacle.

### Why the spectacle does not appear here

1. **Wrong unit of motion.**
   - Each phone is a static DOM page (an app clone), and every visible change is a React state swap that mounts DOM.
   - Mounting is the cost, so every rate had to be throttled per phone: about 1 post per 1.4 s and 1 app change per 6–40 s.
   - A spectacle needs several visible motions per phone per second. Under this architecture the budget rules that out.
   - SCC mobiles/1 works differently. It builds a whole session per scene once (panels and shots: navigation, flicks, taps) and plays it on the Web Animations timeline. React touches each phone rarely, and the compositor carries all motion (`scc/components/mobiles/1/ios/storyboard-auto.tsx`, `phone/stage.tsx`).
2. **The goldfish event was invisible.**
   - A bubble strike became "one more post in a feed". The post landed off-view or among others, so no phone visibly reacted.
   - A strike should be a phone action you can see at once: a banner drops, is tapped, the app opens and navigates, a like lands.
3. **Feeds never scrolled.**
   - The `NaN` bug in Performance left every feed static from the 2026-10-27 performance pass until the end.
   - What the user saw moving was only app hops, card swipes and pop-ins: discrete events on still pages.
   - The fps readings that seemed to prove the performance were of that still field.
4. **Each transition was a one-off CSS keyframe on a whole app layer, and that caused flashes:**
   - opacity ramps from 0;
   - a dark wallpaper showing between two white apps during the "home zoom";
   - lazily loaded images popping in as they scrolled into view;
   - a feed yanked back to the top on every new post.
5. **Layered rate limiters instead of choreography.** Each layer was added for performance, and together they made each phone change rarely and abruptly:
   - the screen's `dwellMs`;
   - the pc's `updatesPerSec`, `hopsPerSec` and `bannersPerSec`;
   - jitter on top of each.
6. **Verified by numbers, not by watching.** Fps and mounts a second were measured, while the motion was not watched over time. A second measuring tab also distorted the early readings.

### Attempts and why each failed

| Attempt | Result |
|---|---|
| Relay-owned slots, one fish per slot, phone dark when its fish let go | read as "dead or alive, neither"; every tuning needed a relay restart |
| Screen-owned slots (`slots.mjs`), 380 fish at ~6 per slot, queue and 150 ms dwell | data side fine and kept; the pc still showed about 1 change per phone per 1.4 s |
| New post on top: feed glides back to the top, post pops in (WAAPI, opacity 0.2→1) | a yank and a blink on every update |
| `transform: scale` on each phone | 10 fps at 32 phones; replaced by CSS `zoom` |
| Random app hops (cube flip, switcher, mix) | unintuitive and too fast |
| iOS home-screen zoom (into the icon, wallpaper, out of the next icon), 650 ms | the dark wallpaper between apps read as a flash |
| Netflix flipped every second, Tinder remounted per card | remounted whole apps; Netflix was then frozen still, which looked dead |
| Last pass: endless conveyor with scroll anchoring, iOS banners, a per-phone app-change clock (~10/s over the grid) mixing open-from-banner, home-bar slide and switcher, nothing appearing in the glass except by motion | judged a failure before it was watched; the `NaN` bug was found at the very end; with scrolling working, the page ran at 51.8 fps, p95 33 ms, without the pace the user wanted |

### For the next agent (`pc/mobiles/2`)

- **Start a new serial.** Copy what is useful; never import from this folder.
  - Keep: `transport/feed/1` (shared by design), the clones' static markup in `apps/`, and `content/events.ts`.
  - Drop: the pacing and transition layers in `phone/`.
- **Design the motion first.**
  - Each phone runs a continuous, scripted session, in the manner of SCC mobiles/1's storyboard: a WAAPI timeline of navigation, scrolls and taps, built once per scene.
  - A goldfish strike is an interrupt that schedules visible actions on that timeline. It is not a data insertion.
- **Make the budget allow the spectacle.**
  - If 64 live DOM apps cannot carry several motions per phone per second, render each app screen to a bitmap once and move bitmaps on the GPU. Read [GPU safety](../../../../../docs/harness/webgpu-tsl-particles.md) first.
  - Measure with feeds actually moving: check that a transform is written.
- **Acceptance, judged on a 30 s recording of the live route with bubble/7 running:**
  - no region of any glass changes without motion;
  - every strike is visible as an action on a phone within about 200 ms;
  - every phone changes app or screen every few seconds;
  - 60 fps at 64 phones with every phone in motion.
