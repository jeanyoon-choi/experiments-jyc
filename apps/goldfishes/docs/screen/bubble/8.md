## 금붕어 1,000 (2026-11-03)

The user asked for up to 1,000 fish with no cost to the frame. The 수 slider now goes to 1,000 (`MAX_FISH`); the opening school stays 380. The fish's look and behaviour are unchanged:

- **The step is cheaper and gives the same result.** A page whose whole hold circle lies under its neighbours is found once per step, from the arcs they cover (`buried()`), so it is neither scored nor searched. The fish skip pages they could never reach. A deterministic benchmark's state hash is the same as before (800 pages, 380 fish). CPU per step went from 10.9 to 2.7 ms at 380 fish, and is 6.7 ms at 1,000.
- **The step runs in a worker** (`fish/school-thread.ts`, `fish/school-worker.ts`). The frame loop uses `SchoolThread` as it used `GoldfishSchool`. Each frame's discs, ranks, links and bubble colours go to the worker, and the fish come back packed. Buffers are transferred, not copied. The fish drawn are one frame behind the bubbles. Without a worker (in node tests, or if the worker fails to load), the same step runs on the main thread. A test checks that this path matches the school stepped directly, fish for fish. The console logs once when the worker starts.
- **The scene draws more cheaply.** Matrices are written straight into the instance buffers, and only the fish there are uploaded. Body and fin colours are one buffer each, shared across their parts. Below body scale 0.78, a lighter geometry is drawn. Its outline lies within 0.13 px of the full one's at the opening size 0.65, and its vertices are shared.

Measured by the lead with every other tab closed (2026-11-03, MacBook Air, Chrome, opening look 맥시멀 믹스 with every group on, 600 frames each): 380 fish median 16.7 ms, p95 17.5, p99 17.6, max 17.7, no frame over 20 ms; 1,000 fish exactly the same. The helper is `scratchpad/measure-fish.js` of that session (`__setFish`, `__frames`).

### The disc the fish keep out of (2026-11-04)

The opening is now 오브제 크기 1.2 and 버블 면적 0.55. At that size, irregular objects reach well past the bubble disc the fish avoided, so fish swam over them. Each baked field now records its `outline`, the furthest point of its shape in any direction. Measuring in any direction keeps it the same however the object is turned. For each page, the object pass keeps last frame's `size × outline` (the larger of the two shapes during a morph) in `pageExtent`. `SchoolThread.fill` multiplies the disc it already copies to the worker by that value. Nothing new runs per fish or per frame beyond one multiplication per page, and every avoidance rule (exclusion, buried, exposed angle, touches) now works on the drawn outline. The disc is a circle, so a long shape leaves some empty space around it rather than overlap.

### Turns never jump (2026-11-04)

The user saw a few objects snap to a new angle. A probe recorded each page's turn from frame to frame over 20 s and found three causes, up to 3.1 rad in a single frame:

- A page's heading is the direction of its velocity. That direction can swing round when the page slows or is shoved.
- Heads lean a little the way they move, clamped near upright. The clamp flipped sides where the heading wrapped round ±π.
- The clamp switched on or off in one frame when a morph went into or out of a head.

Now heads lean by a sine of the heading, eased in and out with the morph. Each page's drawn turn (`pageTurn` in the object pass) springs towards its target along the shorter way round (`TURN_SPRING` 16 per second, `TURN_DAMPING` 0.75). The first version used plain exponential easing (0.2 rad in the first frame of a half turn); the user found it lost its snap and asked for a slightly brisker, springier feel. The user then asked for a higher top speed, as long as it never reads as a jump. With the spring at 16, a half turn starts at 0.22 rad in its first frame and peaks near 23 rad/s, 22° a frame at 60 fps. It is done in 0.3 s, overshooting by about 1°. That costs one atan2 per page per frame. Portions' headings already followed their curve continuously.

### 전파 (2026-11-04)

Every page that catches it fades again after 20 s on average: `keep` 20, where bubble/7 kept it for ever. The spreading tests set `keep: 0` explicitly.
