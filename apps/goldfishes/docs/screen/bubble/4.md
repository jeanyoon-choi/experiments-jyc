## Lifecycle (2026-10-22)

`fish/lifecycle.ts` holds the parameters and pure functions. The school (`fish/goldfish.ts`) steps them, and the scene (`fish/goldfish-scene.ts`) reads each fish's colour and pose. The 생애주기 panel (hourglass) sets the parameters.

**How a fish lives.** A fish lives on the time it spends watching content:

- Every second beside a page wears down its strength by 1 / its lifespan.
- Swimming about without watching wears it down too, at a share of that rate (보지 않고 헤엄칠 때 닳는 정도).
- Lifespans differ between fish (log-uniform, 수명의 개체차).
- A fish never eats the bubbles, and nobody is born of anybody.

**Colour tells its life.**

1. It arrives in its goldfish palette colour.
2. Beside a bubble it eases toward that bubble's coat, as the shader picks it, and takes on more of it while it watches (본 구슬 색으로 물드는 속도). Fins keep a little more of their own colour.
3. Below 회색이 되기 시작하는 남은 기력 it turns quickly to a pale grey and slows to about half speed.

**Death.** Spent, it lets go of its page and stops swimming. It has no tail beat, no schooling, no walls, and gives no attention to the web. Its last motion glides away (0.6 s) into a fall down the screen with a slight sway. It rolls onto its side and grows smaller, and its colour is half lost in the liquid's (배경) as it goes deeper. Once wholly off the bottom it is gone.

**Replacement.** Every 새로 오는 주기 seconds, as many fish as have gone swim in from all the screen's edges, as the school does when the page opens.

The first school's strengths are staggered (0.35–1), so its deaths do not come all at once. 끔 is bubble/3's immortal school.

**Defaults:** lifespan 30 s, idle wear 0.1, spread 0.4, tint 0.8/s, grey from 0.3, sink 55 px/s, a wave every 6 s.

A first set (lifespan 14 s, sink 26 px/s) left more spent fish sinking than living ones, littered over the raft; the defaults were changed after that capture.

Checked in the browser over a minute, with a blue coat to see the tint (a GIF was recorded). There are tests for wear, colour, off, and sinking and waves.

## Falling bodies, crushing, performance (2026-10-22)

**The dead were not visible** (user). They had passed over the bubbles while shrinking and fading into the liquid's colour. Now a spent fish:

- falls like a body: its glide dies away and it accelerates down the screen to 떨어지는 속도 (default 90 px/s);
- slides round the bubbles it cannot pass through, with a sideways push if one stops it;
- stays full size and grey until it leaves the bottom of the screen;
- slips through after 3 s if it is held still (shut in a cavity of the raft).

**Crushed.** A fish dies where a page's bubble swells or trades places and comes more than 버블에 밀려 죽는 거리 (default 20 px, 0 off) over it in one step. Travelling portions sweeping past do not count.

A first test measured how far a fish was moved in a step instead. That killed nearly a whole school in the opening seconds, because swimming into a bubble and being nudged by portions counted.

A fish shut in a cavity of the raft also dies, instead of jumping to the edge.

**Performance, measured with `?measure`** (ms per frame, median / 95th percentile, including GPU sync; Brave on the MacBook Air, 380 fish):

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| School step | 1.6–3.1 ms |
| Fish draw | 0.3 ms |
| Frame cost, bubble/3 | 14–22 / 15–30 ms |
| Frame cost, bubble/4 with the lifecycle on | 15–31 / 18–37 ms |
| Frame cost, bubble/4 with the lifecycle off | about the same as on |
| Frame cost, bubble/4 with 0 fish | 14–15 ms |

The differences between runs come from the raft's layout, which varies with each run, not from the lifecycle.

The lifecycle's own work is allocation-free (velocities and colours written in place). Fish tail phases are pruned only every 120 frames, and palette colours are parsed once.

## Grey and falling at once (2026-10-22)

A fish now turns grey the moment it dies and starts falling at once (user). Before, it greyed over its last strength and glided before falling.

- **Defaults:** 죽기 전 미리 회색이 되는 남은 기력 0 (the slider can bring the early greying back); 떨어지는 속도 170 px/s (range up to 500).
- **The fall:** starts at a third of full speed, reaches full speed in about 0.2 s, and the sideways glide dies away quickly.
- **The body:** the tail stops within 0.3 s, and it rolls onto its side within 0.8 s.

## Performance and fish spacing (2026-10-22)

**Where a frame goes.** `?measure` now breaks a frame down into `window.bubbleLaps` (ms, smoothed; MacBook Air, 1,000 pages, 380 fish):

| Part | ms |
|---|---|
| Web model | 0.5–0.8 |
| Raft layout (`arrange`, the same as bubble/3) | 3.5–4.4 |
| Bubbles emitted | 0.2 |
| Fish | 2.2–2.6 |
| Raft draw, CPU | 0.3 |
| Raft draw, GPU | 6.3–6.8 |
| Fish draw, GPU | 2.2 |

Frames run at a steady 60 per second: frame interval median 16.7 ms, worst 17.7 ms over 8 s.

**Fish choosing a page.** A fish choosing a page by salience used to look for an open hold point at every page that beat the best score so far, walking through all 1,000. It now scores every page first and searches from the best down, stopping at the first reachable one. The choice is the same (same page, same angle, lowest index among equals; a deterministic benchmark's state hash is unchanged). The step went from 2.7 to 2.0 ms.

**Fish no longer overlap** (user), living or dead.

- **Body shape:** each body is a capsule matching the drawn fish from above. It runs along the heading from 2.4 ahead of the centre to 7.6 behind, with radius 3.6 (glyph units × scale). Fish touch where they are drawn touching; a circle round each would hold them apart at the sides and let them overlap nose to tail.
- **Separation:** after the fish move, overlapping pairs are pushed apart equally, and the part of their velocities that closes them stops (two sweeps over the 64 px buckets). This happens again after the bubbles push fish, and the bubbles are resolved once more for the fish that moved, so the bubbles still win.
- **Measured** (benchmark, 380 fish): overlapping pairs went from 340 (234 by more than half) to 3 (none by more than half). It costs about 0.2 ms.

**Tried and dropped.**

- A 2D grid instead of the layout's sweep along x (result identical, but 2.5 times slower: the raft is dense, so a 3 × 3 cell query sees more candidates than the sweep's strip).
- Hoisting the layout's live reads into locals (no gain).
