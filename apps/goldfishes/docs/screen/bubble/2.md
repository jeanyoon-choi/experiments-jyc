Every bubble can be a lobe of Jeff Koons's *Balloon Dog*: mirror-polished stainless steel under a transparent colour coat. 비주얼 → 재질 switches between 비눗막 (bubble/1's film, unchanged) and 쿤스, the default here; 코팅 picks 마젠타 (the default), 옐로, 블루, 오렌지, 레드, 스틸 (bare steel) or 섞음 (each bubble one of the five coats).

What the material copies, from 27 photographs of the five dogs (e.g. Magenta at Versailles: [1](https://live.staticflickr.com/3056/3054111372_96cf7513b6_b.jpg), [2](https://live.staticflickr.com/3203/3075259150_6ef41d4862_b.jpg); Yellow on the Met roof: [3](https://live.staticflickr.com/3616/3469645017_860291f51c_b.jpg); Blue at LACMA and the Broad: [4](https://live.staticflickr.com/6077/6059855238_858dd091fa_b.jpg), [5](https://live.staticflickr.com/4159/33795582783_078a2df8df_b.jpg); Magenta in a white gallery: [6](https://live.staticflickr.com/3758/19635770999_16124e469c_b.jpg)) and, as counter-examples, a glazed ceramic imitation and a generic metal render:

- no diffuse light at all: the form is read only through the room it reflects; the coat tints that reflection, so bright things come back pale and tinted, dark things deep and dense;
- the room: a bright ceiling with converging light strips and lamps (small clipped highlights), white walls and tall windows broken by a dark band of visitors and frames — the horizon that bends round every lobe — and a pale floor brightening towards the plinth;
- each lobe turned and tipped a little (the dog's legs, body and ears point different ways), so horizons and windows never repeat; a crisp plinth band, physically right on a sphere, read as a row of smiles and was removed;
- round inflated lobes pinched into dark creases where they press against a neighbour (the twists and clefts), the steel there reflecting the neighbour's coat again;
- a clear top coat that reflects untinted at grazing angles, and a roll-off that takes highlights towards white.

The reference photographs were only studied, not added to the app. 60 fps at DPR 2 on the MacBook Air, as with the film.

## Rebuilt from the photographs (2026-10-20)

The first version reflected a crisp, graphic room — rows of windows, figures, ceiling strips, a plinth band — identically in every bubble: it read as a texture printed on balls, flat and cheap. Studied again at full resolution, the photographs show something else: most of each lobe is a smooth, deep gradient of its coat; the room is soft and barely legible; light arrives as long soft streaks (tall windows stretched along the curve) and a few tiny clipped points (lamps); and lobes mirror one another, which is why no two look alike.

Now:

- each pixel's reflected ray is traced against the neighbouring bubbles as spheres; a hit shows that lobe — its coat and its own reflection of the room — so every bubble's look comes from its own neighbourhood;
- the room is broad light only: bright above, dimmer at the horizon and in the room behind the camera, a pale floor brightening towards the plinth, soft window streaks, sparse tiny lamps, a faint soft darkening for visitors; it is read from the 3D direction, so there is no azimuth seam through the lobes' centres;
- each lobe is turned, tipped and rolled (as the dog's legs, tail and ears point different ways), so its horizon belt tilts and its streaks fall differently;
- lobes stay round to the edge and meet in a narrow dark cleft (a few pixels at any size; the earlier wide crease read as machined bevels on large bubbles), with occlusion there and the neighbour's reflection around it;
- a filmic (ACES) curve keeps dark reflections dense in the coat and rolls highlights to white.

Checked in the browser before and after, with large bubbles in view; 60 fps at DPR 2.
