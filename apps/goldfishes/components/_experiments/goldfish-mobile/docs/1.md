## What it is

The work's own goldfish in a white studio, holding by its edges a mobile three times too large for it. The mobile stands upright, square to the ground, and its screen runs a vertical feed of short clips: each is held for as long as attention holds (most 0.9–2.5 s, about a tenth flicked past in under 0.7 s, about a tenth stayed on for 3.5–6 s and sometimes liked), then the next slides up in 0.26 s as the right fin strokes. Two arrangements, switched at the bottom right (user, 2026-11-13): **한 마리**, and **10 × 10**, a hundred fish in a grid on the same ground, each at its own mobile. Drag to look round.

## How it is made

- **The fish** (`fish/goldfish.ts`): the sign of `components/bubble/9/fish/goldfish-scene.ts` — an ellipsoid body, a peduncle, a forked tail, a dorsal and two side fins, two black eyes, the same outlines, sizes and palettes — kept, and made as an object (user, after seeing it copied as it was: "너무 이상함 … 조금 더 hyperreal 한걸로"):
  - the body and peduncle are one seamless volume under a glaze (clearcoat over a smooth colour);
  - the fins are the same outlines with a rounded edge, in tinted glass (transmission) for one fish and plain gloss for a hundred;
  - the eyes are polished and sit on the sides of the head (over the raft they sit on top, for a view from above).
  - Nothing is drawn on it: no scales, no rays. One fish is the 금붕어 palette; a school is dealt the six palettes 섞음 uses.
  - Every part is an `InstancedMesh`, so one fish or a hundred are five draws. Per frame only the tails' and side fins' matrices are written.
- **The mobiles** (`mobile/`): instanced dark metal and glass slabs, 0.705 × 1.5 world units against a fish about 1.2 long. A screen is a 2D canvas drawn every frame as a `CanvasTexture` (sRGB, no mipmaps) on a `MeshBasicMaterial` with `toneMapped: false`. One fish has one feed at 468 × 1014 and a `RectAreaLight` of its mean colour shining on the fish; the grid shares six feeds at half that resolution, each on its own clock, and has no screen lamps.
  - The clips are the photographs `pc/mobiles/2` uses (`public/assets/goldfishes/pc-mobiles-2`: licensed stock and model-made pictures, no real people), each filling the screen and slowly pushed in. They are stills, not video.
  - No captions or names (text is avoided): the feed's chrome is its buttons only.
- **Studio** (`scene.ts`): `RoomEnvironment` for reflections, one key with a soft shadow, the ground the canvas's clear colour, exactly `#fff`; no post-processing.
- The screen-in-3D choice follows a reading of 37 official three.js examples. CSS3D was not used because DOM always sits behind or in front of the whole WebGL picture, so a fin could not cross the screen.

## Failure: a newly built "realistic" goldfish (2026-11-13, removed the same day)

The first version did not use the work's goldfish. It built a new one from anatomy (a lofted body, a computed scale relief and colour map, rayed translucent fins, a clearcoat and part-mirror material) and held a life-size mobile low under its chin, tilted. The user: "이따구로 할꺼면 집어치워라 — 이미 금붕어 모델 만들어논게 있는데 왜 그거 안써"; "너가 방금 만든게 내가 피하라고 한 exactly cheesy situation"; the mobile should stand square to the ground, may be exaggerated and larger, and its screen must read well.

- The lead had found `goldfish-scene.ts` and set it aside as "not hyper-real", then searched the web for a model and built one. The request's "hyperreal 3D 금붕어모델들" meant the fish this work already has.
- A hand-built imitation of a real animal reads as an illustration of one. That is the same failure as bubble/9's first catalogue and 금붕어의 천적.
- Rule taken: use the work's existing signs as they are; before making a new model of something the work already draws, ask.

## Not good enough yet

- The opening views are from behind the fish, so the screens are seen about 45° off their axis and no face is seen.
- The fins touch the mobile's edges but do not wrap them.
- One fish's glass fins read as flat orange more than as glass.
- Stills stand in for video. In the grid six feeds repeat across a hundred screens.
- Only the opening views were looked at, for a few seconds each.
