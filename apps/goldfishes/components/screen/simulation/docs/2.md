## What the artist decided (2026-11-25)

1. "With no input the screen stays" means **no new bubble appears. The circulation goes on.**
2. **The disposition (기질, note 1115) is at the centre of the model now.**
3. **The disposition is the bubble's, not the sign's.** It has nothing to do with the objects, whose vocabulary will all change. For now **simple parameters, to see the feeling, replaceable later**; nothing semantic ("overengineering하거나 semantic하게 만드는 데 highly skeptical").
4. A stroke's bubble comes in at a bubble's mean share.
5. A new home, `simulation/2`.

## The model, in one sentence

Attention keeps flowing between bubbles as in `bubble/9`; nothing in it is thrown by dice; a bubble that holds too much tires, bursts and recovers; a bubble that changes takes the disposition and the object of the bubble that sends it the most; a goldfish goes by its own taste and tires of a disposition; new bubbles come only from a finger drawn across the screen.

## What is bubble/9's, unchanged

The circulation itself (one conserved weight, moved along links as flow bubbles; `web/iteration.ts`), the links and how they follow appeal, appeal growing with size, bubble/6's tiring, the goldfish's body, swimming, lifecycle and deaths, watching raising a bubble's quality and turning it, the raft, materials, objects, morph, sound, panels, presets A1–B4, 전파, 랩.

**The disposition** is `rules/disposition.ts`: a direction on a circle, two numbers; `match` is the cosine. **Replace that one file to put another kind of disposition in.** A bubble's is given by its number at the opening; a goldfish's by its own number when it is born, fixed for its life (no rule that it grows like what it watches: left out as unnecessary). The object a bubble shows is its *face* (`rules/bubbles.ts`): it means nothing to the disposition; the two only travel together.

## What took the dice's place: nothing new

**Taking the dice out stilled the raft** (below: of the ten largest, 1.71 replaced a second → 0.01–0.13; the flow bubbles to a sixth). What the lead had expected to replace them, the goldfish and their dispositions, did not: even with each fish's watching counting twelve times more, 0.2–0.7.

What does move it without dice is **bubble/6's own cycle, already in bubble/9**: a bubble that holds more than so many fair shares tires, bursts and recovers. In bubble/9 it starts at 80 shares and recovers over 10 s, values set with the dice on; there a giant sits just under the line for good. Started lower, the cycle runs by itself: a bubble swells because the large draw more, tires because it held too much, bursts, and what it sent on swells in its turn. No rule was added. Three of bubble/9's own controls have other values while the dice are off, shown as sliders under question 1:

Where the tiring starts trades liveliness against the gap between large and small (no dice, 30 s, 1; all four changes): at 40 shares 1.7–2.0 replaced a second and a largest width 16.2 times the median; at 50, 1.4–1.5 and 18.0; at 60, 1.1–1.2 and 19.7. bubble/6's accepted range was 17–22; 50 is the opening.

**Ten minutes without input** (step 4, tiring at 40): it does not still and does not run away — 1.7–2.0 replaced a second and 500–600 flow bubbles in every minute of the ten; the leading object holds 22–37% of the area and is overtaken; 9–12 objects hold over 1% each throughout. **But the number of different objects on the screen falls steadily, 228 → 110 in ten minutes** (a bubble that changes takes an object already there, and nothing new comes without a stroke): the many small ones thin out first. Not run longer.

**One stroke** (nine bubbles in a line), at eight different moments of the same run (`AT=60 node …/study/ladder.ts answer`): its object's largest share of the raft's area was 17–47% (reached 8–57 s after the stroke), and a minute after the stroke it held 0.2–25%. The same stroke is answered differently each time; none took the whole raft; in these eight none failed outright. The goldfish crushed in the six seconds after such a stroke were no more than in the six before (2–22 against 4–18). **A longer stroke with the objects' reach counted (17 bubbles, every object taken to reach 1.5 times its disc, as the browser does with each object's own) does crush them at some places**: 5 and 7 after strokes at one place, 26 and 52 at another (5–16 before); the size the bubbles come in at is not the cause (as many at a twentieth of a share).

**What the dispositions themselves change is small in these numbers.** The ten-minute picture with taste 0 and tiring by the one bubble is the same within the spread (1.5–1.7 replaced a second, the leader 30–35%). With tiring by the one bubble the same eight strokes peaked at 15–28% (by disposition: 17–47%); a minute later two of the eight still held ground either way (9–13%; by disposition 13–25%). Taste at 3 instead of 1: no difference seen. So the disposition is in place and replaceable, but **what is seen on the screen now is made by the tiring cycle, the taking-on of a neighbour's object, and the strokes**, not by the dispositions' values.

Deaths, in this harness (which kills more than the browser does, for bubble/9 too): bubble/9 700 a minute, simulation/2 611.

## Known shortfalls

1. **Objects thin out without strokes** (above). If that is not wanted, question 3's left answer (a bubble's own next object) keeps bubble/9's variety and gives up the spreading of an object.
2. **A stroke can take nearly half the raft for some seconds** (17–47% in eight; 45% once in the browser), and can also fail (once in the browser). Its starting appeal moves both ends together (at 1.7: 1–40%); the size of an answer is the system's, not the stroke's.
3. **The dispositions' values change little** (above). They decide who goes where; they do not yet make the picture.
4. The largest bubble is 18 times the median's width against bubble/9's 24.
5. **A stroke can crush 30–60 goldfish at once** (seen twice in the browser, reproduced without a screen at some places and not others). The bubbles are placed as 전파's finger-skating places them in bubble/9 (`raft.createPage`); where a stroke crosses large bubbles the raft shoves bubbles aside within a step, which is what crushes. Not looked into further; not fixed.
6. Performance: see below. What is left is bubble/9's own — the layout step on the frame's thread, the GPU by the pixel, the opening's bakes.

## Performance (2026-11-25, artist: "optimise performance")

Measured first. Nothing below changes a number of the model or a pixel: the ladder's output for the same run is the same to the last digit before and after, and the 97 tests pass unchanged.

**Found:**

- **The frame is held up by its own thread, not by the pixels.** With the canvas at 767 × 383 (`?ratio=0.6`) the frame's work still took 17 ms. The same layout step (`raft.arrange`) that takes 1.8 ms in node took 4.6 ms in the browser on a quiet machine and 9–10 ms while the objects were being baked.
- **The three bake workers are the cause of the second half of that.** Their requests dropped by hand in the open page: `arrange` 10.0 → 6.4 ms, the web's step 1.55 → 0.63 ms, within seconds.
- **They were never idle.** For the first 80 s all three are full (some 600 bakes of 0.5–0.7 s). After that 5–7 requests a second go on for good — bubbles swell, burst and take each other's objects, so some 70 kinds trade the finer pools' slots every 30 s — and one in three was of a kind a worker no longer kept: baked over again, 0.5 s each, 0.6–1.1 cores without end.
- **The canvas stepped down for nothing, and that made it worse.** `pace` waited 30 s and then judged frames that were slow only because of the bakes: on the lead's machine the canvas went from 2 to 1.4 device px at about 34 s, every time, and each step bakes every object again.
- **A goldfish that looks about asked whether each of the 800 bubbles is buried**, every step: 31% of all the CPU of the loop (in the school's worker).

**Changed:**

| | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The school's step (`fish/attention.ts`, `fish/obstacles.ts`): whether a bubble is buried is worked out only when it is the best one left; the arcs sorted without a call per comparison | `buried` 31% of the loop's CPU | 2%; the school's part of the CPU about halved; the same fish to the last digit |
| `pace` (`loop/frame-loop.ts`) judges no frame drawn while a bake is under way | canvas 1791 × 895 from 34 s on | stays 2558 × 1278 |
| Bake workers by the machine's cores (`objects/fields/bake-queue.ts`): one up to four cores, two up to six, else three (was: cores − 1, at most three) | — | not measured on such a machine: the lead's has ten |
| A worker keeps 40 kinds' bakes where the machine reports 8 GB or more, else 24 as before (`objects/fields/tier-data.ts`; 116 MB a worker at most) | 37–54 re-bakes in 30 s | 53–63 in 42 s: a fifth fewer, not gone |

The same window before and after (1279 × 639 CSS px, two minutes in, an automation window behind other windows on a machine at load 6–9: read the comparison, not the numbers): **47.6 fps at 1791 × 895 → 55–57 fps at 2558 × 1278**; the frame's own work 16.5 → 12.4 ms (median); frames over 20 ms 23% → 7%.

**Tried and dropped:** `Math.hypot` written out (bit-identical, and no faster in this V8).

**Not done:**

- The layout step is still the largest thing on the frame's thread. Its seven contact sweeps cannot be reordered without changing every position (they move the bubbles as they go); fewer sweeps would be cheaper and would show (bubbles pressed further into each other): the artist's to decide.
- The re-bakes are fewer, not gone (0.6–0.9 of a core, on and off). Keeping every kind's bake would end them and cost about 670 MB.
- The opening's 80 s of bakes: the same every time the page opens; keeping them between visits (the browser's cache) would end that.
- The GPU's share was not ranked (`?measure` cannot: its sync waits on the swap). A coarser canvas is the lever there (`?ratio=1.4`).
- Nothing was measured on the Windows computer. To find out there: open `/screen/simulation/2?measure`, wait two minutes, read `bubbleLaps` in the console.

## 라이브 화면: a webcam in the room (2026-11-26, a trial)

Artist: keep the photographed room as the light, and hang the webcam's picture in it as one bright screen; a panel option; a mobile over WebRTC later, so the source is its own module.

- **What it is.** One more thing the room holds, beside the key's softbox: a lit rectangle on the camera's side (straight towards the viewer, whatever the tilt), showing the live picture, mirrored. `envAt` adds it, so every material that reflects the room reflects it. The room's light is untouched.
- **Panel.** 비주얼 → 라이브 화면: 없음 · 웹캠, and a fold with 화면 밝기 (`liveStrength`, 2) and 화면 크기 (`liveSize`, 0.4). It stays as set through every 연출 (`looks/apply.ts`); 처음 값으로 turns it off.
- **Code.** `render/live/source.ts`: a `LiveSource` over any `MediaStream` (`streamSource`), the camera as one (`cameraSource`), and `LIVE_SOURCES` by number. A mobile's stream is one more entry there; nothing else changes. `render/assets.ts` draws each new frame to the stills' third layer (512²) and rebuilds its mips; `render/shading.ts` `liveAt` reads it.
- **Seen** (lead, with a stand-in stream: the page's own portrait moving on a canvas, since the automation browser has no camera): on 크롬 a small upright face on the parts of an object that face the viewer, and skin-coloured streaks where the surface turns; at 화면 크기 1 the face spreads into streaks over large objects. On 맥시멀 믹스 nothing readable. No real camera, and no dim room, has been tried.
- **Cost** (same tab, off/on twice, 7 s each, a loaded machine): the renderer's CPU lap 1.2 → 3.2 ms and 2.3 → 6.0 ms; frames 34.9 → 33.7 and 24.4 → 21.6 per second. The 2D canvas between the video and the texture is the likely cost (unverified); uploading at 256², or a GPU blit, are the things to try if it stays.
- **Not done.** No face tracking (the picture is the camera's whole frame). 젤 · 세럼's own mirror (`serum.ts`) reads the room directly and does not show the screen. A refused permission shows nothing and says nothing.

## A stroke always lands (2026-11-26)

Artist: past 200 laid bubbles a stroke did nothing — the web holds 1,000 and opens with 800, and nothing was ever taken out. Decided: at the cap, a stroke's bubble takes the place of the smallest bubble on show (the one that has lost the most attention, so the one least missed; no ages kept).

- `web/model.ts` `renewPage`: the page drops every link from it and to it and starts as a newly added page would. `model/pagerank.ts` `renew`: what it was shown at goes back to the rest (the total stays 1). `raft/raft.ts` `takeOver`, used by `lay` only when `createPage` returns null: the smallest shown, never the stroke's own last bubble.
- Its number stays, so the school, the renderer and the bakes see a bubble that moved and changed, nothing more.
- Checked: at 1,000 pages a renewal leaves no link to the page, ranks summing to 1, and ten seconds of stepping finite. In the browser, fourteen strokes across the field (about 320 bubbles at one per 30 px) and then one down the middle: the last stroke's objects appear along its path.
- **Corrected the same day** (artist: after a new stroke the objects morph at once and are often not the same object). Because the number stays, the object pass kept the page's morph and flowed out of whatever stood there into the stroke's object over the morph's length, so a stroke at the cap read as many objects. Now `loop/frame-loop.ts` `stroke` tells the object pass to forget a page that was taken over (`objects/pass/page-morphs.ts` `forget`: shown as the stroke's object at once, as a new page is) and the watching to start it from nothing (`simulation/attention.ts` `renew`). Checked in the browser at 1,000 bubbles: a stroke of 35 bubbles, `strokeBubbles` 35–37 in each of the nine seconds after it (the model kept one object throughout; the same stroke went from 3% to 35% of the area in those seconds). The picture before the correction was not captured, and in the captures after it the stroke's bubbles could not be told apart inside the raft; at the stroke's ends outside the raft they were plain spheres of different colours just after it was drawn, not explained.
- Not looked at: goldfish that were watching the bubble taken over (they are left watching a bubble that is now elsewhere).
