## What the artist decided (2026-11-25)

1. "With no input the screen stays" means **no new bubble appears. The circulation goes on.**
2. **The disposition (기질, note 1115) is at the centre of the model now.**
3. **The disposition is the bubble's, not the sign's.** It has nothing to do with the objects, whose vocabulary will all change. For now **simple parameters, to see the feeling, replaceable later**; nothing semantic ("overengineering하거나 semantic하게 만드는 데 highly skeptical").
4. A stroke's bubble comes in at a bubble's mean share.
5. A new home, `simulation/2`.

## The model, in one sentence

Attention keeps flowing between bubbles as in `bubble/9`; nothing in it is thrown by dice; a bubble that holds too much tires, bursts and recovers; a bubble that changes takes the disposition and the object of the bubble that sends it the most; a goldfish goes by its own taste and tires of a disposition; new bubbles come only from a finger drawn across the screen.

## What is bubble/9's, unchanged

The circulation itself (one conserved weight, moved along links as flow bubbles; `web/iteration.ts`), the links and how they follow appeal, appeal growing with size, bubble/6's tiring, the goldfish's body, swimming, lifecycle and deaths, watching raising a bubble's quality and turning it, the raft, materials, objects, morph, sound, panels, presets A1–B4, 전파, 랩.

## The four places where it leaves bubble/9

Each is a question in the 시스템 panel (`rules/versions.ts`); the left answer of every one is bubble/9's, and the chip **bubble/9** sets them all there.

| | bubble/9 | simulation/2 | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 A bubble's quality | wanders by itself (dice on each of 800 bubbles, every beat) | rises only while goldfish watch it, and sinks back | `raft/raft.ts` `own.drift` |
| 2 What a goldfish goes by | a fixed value per fish and bubble (0.8–1.2); tires of the one bubble it watched | its own disposition against the bubble's (pull × e^(taste × match)); tires of a disposition, wherever it meets it | `fish/attention.ts` (`leaning`, `watch`) |
| 3 What a watched bubble changes to | its own next object in a list | the disposition **and** the object of the bubble that sends it the most this beat | `rules/bubbles.ts` `adopt`, `raft.sender` |
| 4 New bubbles | 0.85 a second by themselves | only a stroke: a bubble every 30 px of a finger's way, one new disposition and one new object for the whole stroke | `raft.lay`, `loop/frame-loop.ts` `stroke`, `index.tsx` |

**The disposition** is `rules/disposition.ts`: a direction on a circle, two numbers; `match` is the cosine. **Replace that one file to put another kind of disposition in.** A bubble's is given by its number at the opening; a goldfish's by its own number when it is born, fixed for its life (no rule that it grows like what it watches: left out as unnecessary). The object a bubble shows is its *face* (`rules/bubbles.ts`): it means nothing to the disposition; the two only travel together.

## What took the dice's place: nothing new

**Taking the dice out stilled the raft** (below: of the ten largest, 1.71 replaced a second → 0.01–0.13; the flow bubbles to a sixth). What the lead had expected to replace them, the goldfish and their dispositions, did not: even with each fish's watching counting twelve times more, 0.2–0.7.

What does move it without dice is **bubble/6's own cycle, already in bubble/9**: a bubble that holds more than so many fair shares tires, bursts and recovers. In bubble/9 it starts at 80 shares and recovers over 10 s, values set with the dice on; there a giant sits just under the line for good. Started lower, the cycle runs by itself: a bubble swells because the large draw more, tires because it held too much, bursts, and what it sent on swells in its turn. No rule was added. Three of bubble/9's own controls have other values while the dice are off, shown as sliders under question 1:

| --- | --- | --- |
| 지치기 시작하는 크기 (네트워크 → 터지는 크기) | 80 | **50** |
| 회복하는 시간 (네트워크 → 회복) | 10 s | **30 s** |
| 금붕어 한 마리의 힘 (금붕어 → 무리 크기 반영) | 0 | **1** |

A stroke's bubble comes in at one mean share with appeal 2 (`놓일 때의 매력`; 전파's newcomers have 1.5), which wears off like any other unless goldfish keep it up.

`node components/screen/simulation/2/study/ladder.ts` (the real loop — web, display, raft, emitter, school, watching — at 1440 × 810, 800 bubbles, 380 goldfish, preset A1's values; means over 110–180 s, no input). Step 0 agrees with bubble/9 measured in the browser.

Where the tiring starts trades liveliness against the gap between large and small (no dice, 30 s, 1; all four changes): at 40 shares 1.7–2.0 replaced a second and a largest width 16.2 times the median; at 50, 1.4–1.5 and 18.0; at 60, 1.1–1.2 and 19.7. bubble/6's accepted range was 17–22; 50 is the opening.

**Ten minutes without input** (step 4, tiring at 40): it does not still and does not run away — 1.7–2.0 replaced a second and 500–600 flow bubbles in every minute of the ten; the leading object holds 22–37% of the area and is overtaken; 9–12 objects hold over 1% each throughout. **But the number of different objects on the screen falls steadily, 228 → 110 in ten minutes** (a bubble that changes takes an object already there, and nothing new comes without a stroke): the many small ones thin out first. Not run longer.

**One stroke** (nine bubbles in a line), at eight different moments of the same run (`AT=60 node …/study/ladder.ts answer`): its object's largest share of the raft's area was 17–47% (reached 8–57 s after the stroke), and a minute after the stroke it held 0.2–25%. The same stroke is answered differently each time; none took the whole raft; in these eight none failed outright. The goldfish crushed in the six seconds after such a stroke were no more than in the six before (2–22 against 4–18). **A longer stroke with the objects' reach counted (17 bubbles, every object taken to reach 1.5 times its disc, as the browser does with each object's own) does crush them at some places**: 5 and 7 after strokes at one place, 26 and 52 at another (5–16 before); the size the bubbles come in at is not the cause (as many at a twentieth of a share).

**What the dispositions themselves change is small in these numbers.** The ten-minute picture with taste 0 and tiring by the one bubble is the same within the spread (1.5–1.7 replaced a second, the leader 30–35%). With tiring by the one bubble the same eight strokes peaked at 15–28% (by disposition: 17–47%); a minute later two of the eight still held ground either way (9–13%; by disposition 13–25%). Taste at 3 instead of 1: no difference seen. So the disposition is in place and replaceable, but **what is seen on the screen now is made by the tiring cycle, the taking-on of a neighbour's object, and the strokes**, not by the dispositions' values.

Deaths, in this harness (which kills more than the browser does, for bubble/9 too): bubble/9 700 a minute, simulation/2 611.

## Seen in the browser (lead, 2026-11-25; 1336 × 785, preset A1)

- Opens on bubble/9's picture: a few dozen large objects among hundreds of small, flow bubbles, 60 fps (16.7 ms median, worst 17.6). No errors.
- Left running for ten minutes without input: still moving at the end (`window.goldfishTrack`).
- Two strokes. The first (18 bubbles, 40 s after opening): 2% of the area, down to 0.4% in half a minute; it did not spread. The second (13 bubbles, nine minutes in): 12% after 5 s, 45% after 11 s, 14–25% over the next half minute, 2% after 50 s, shown on up to 24 bubbles. **Goldfish were crushed at both: about 33 of 380 in the 3–5 s after the first, about 60 in the 3 s round the second** (bubble/9 itself crushes some 160 a minute in this window, [1](./1.md)).
- Beside bubble/9 in the same window: bubble/9 has one or two objects much larger than the rest; simulation/2 has more large objects of nearer size (the table's 18 against 24).
- Not looked at: other presets, 전파, 랩, the sound, a Windows computer.

`window.goldfishTrack` holds a record a second (the last stroke's object: `strokeBubbles`, `strokeArea`); `window.goldfishTrackSummary(60)` its means.

## Known shortfalls

1. **Objects thin out without strokes** (above). If that is not wanted, question 3's left answer (a bubble's own next object) keeps bubble/9's variety and gives up the spreading of an object.
2. **A stroke can take nearly half the raft for some seconds** (17–47% in eight; 45% once in the browser), and can also fail (once in the browser). Its starting appeal moves both ends together (at 1.7: 1–40%); the size of an answer is the system's, not the stroke's.
3. **The dispositions' values change little** (above). They decide who goes where; they do not yet make the picture.
4. The largest bubble is 18 times the median's width against bubble/9's 24.
5. **A stroke can crush 30–60 goldfish at once** (seen twice in the browser, reproduced without a screen at some places and not others). The bubbles are placed as 전파's finger-skating places them in bubble/9 (`raft.createPage`); where a stroke crosses large bubbles the raft shoves bubbles aside within a step, which is what crushes. Not looked into further; not fixed.
6. Performance is bubble/9's: nothing was done for the Windows computer beyond the canvas stepping down when frames are slow (as in simulation/1; not known to help).
