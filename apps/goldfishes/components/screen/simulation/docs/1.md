## What was asked, and what was built instead

**Asked (artist, 2026-11-24):** bubble/9's feeling — a trend spreading, the raft circulating and evolving — produced by an agent-based model in place of PageRank, which is a stand-in algorithm. `simulation/0` (M9) is a reference with problems of its own, not the thing to install.

**Built:** `simulation/0`'s M9 moved rule by rule under bubble/9's body. A low-level integration of the reference, not a model designed for the body.

## What went wrong

1. **The task was misread as a port.** The lead told the artist the formulas need not be understood before going on, then moved M9's rules and values (tuned for 60 bubbles, 2.5 fish a bubble, fish as points that sit anywhere in the packing) into a body of 800 bubbles and 380 fish that can only reach the raft's outer faces. The question that should have come first: what in bubble/9's mathematics makes its feeling, and which agent rule would do each of those jobs.
2. **The body's regulators were removed as "logic".** bubble/6's tiring (a page past 80 times its share bursts), the links' pull that keeps large pages inside the raft, the inflow of new pages, the watched page moving on. With them went the circulation and the bound on one object taking the screen.
3. **M9's resting state was the opening.** Every opening keyword familiar at 0.9, so no fish had a reason to move: 5 fish a second changed bubble, 0.5 objects a second changed against bubble/9's 18.
4. **Only the rim took part.** Size was the fish in contact, and fish reach only outer faces, so a few rim bubbles held 14–25% of the area each and a stroke laid inside the raft drew no fish at all (0% of the watching fish, headless).
5. **A dull screen was written up as "a result of the rules"** and measured further instead of being treated as below standard.

### 에이전트 2: a bubble's size comes from its goldfish (2026-11-25) — DISCARDED by the artist the same day

**The artist: "지금 방법 완전히 폐기해야함, 전혀 bubble/9과 같은 비주얼 아웃풋도 없고." Do not build on it or tune it.** The route opens on 에이전트 1 again, with 200 bubbles. 에이전트 2 stays in the 시스템 panel as the record.

What was wrong with it, as the artist saw it over the afternoon: one bubble swelling and falling with no input and fish drifting to it (called free swimming); one stroke filling the whole screen (a new bubble's pull drew most of the school and the raft was the head count); then, corrected, bubbles too small for anything to be seen; sizes jumping as fish came and went; and nothing of bubble/9's picture at any point. Under it all: the lead left bubble/9's body — a full raft, large objects, flows, morphs — to build a raft up from one bubble, and tuned its own feedback loop at 1–60 bubbles without once setting the result beside bubble/9. **The measure of any rule here is bubble/9's picture on a full raft, side by side, before anything else.**

What follows is the record of what was built.


The artist, on the route opening with one bubble: a newcomer must not come in that large, the whole school must not gather on the one bubble; with few bubbles the fish swim free and only those near a bubble are held, and as the bubbles grow in number more are held and the rich get richer. Not by patch rules. And: not by fixing a bubble's size from outside — the size is the goldfish's.

Three answers, one file each in `rules/` (the questions and versions are `rules/versions.ts`; the 시스템 panel is drawn from it, 에이전트 1 is the model of 11-24 and one chip away):
- **`supply.ts` → watched.** The 15% every bubble does not send on each beat goes to the bubbles the goldfish are at (bubble/9: over every bubble).
- **`scale.ts` → held.** The whole raft is as large as the fish it holds: all 380 held is bubble/9's raft, nobody is one fish's worth. A stroke's bubble comes in at one fish's worth (the ×5 and the 20% cap are 에이전트 1's).
- **`attach.ts` → worth.** A fish takes the bubble that pulls it most only if that pull is over a threshold (`Model.worth` 0.5, a first guess); the size in the pull is the size on the screen. Otherwise it swims free: it wanders (`meander`; before, a fish with no bubble went straight until a wall turned it) and is not worn down (artist: a life is spent on watching).

Watched in the browser (1279 × 639, preset A1), by `window.goldfishTrack` every 10 s:
- **One bubble, no input, 50 s:** 0–15 of 380 held, coming and going (the bubble swells to 4% of the screen and falls back); the rest swim free. No fish died.
- **Three strokes at 55 s (60 bubbles), to 212 s:** held rose to 208 for a moment (new bubbles pull hard) and settled at 50–90, the raft at 13–25% of the screen, the largest bubble 9–17 times the median's width. Deaths in those 150 s: 21 spent, 8 crushed, 20 shut in. bubble/9 in the same window: 293 in 60 s.
- 에이전트 1 ↔ 2 by its chip, on a running screen: seen once each way (the screen starts over when the raft-size answer changes).

**Two mass deaths found on the way, both the lead's.** As first built, 140–200 fish were crushed in the first half second: the raft was measured against the school as counted, which is 0 until the school's first step comes back, so for that moment it was bubble/9's size over fish coming in. The lead had looked for twenty seconds and counted tests; the artist saw it ("자유 유영이 아니라 그냥 떼죽음"). Now measured against the school asked for.

**How this route is checked from now on** (the artist, same day: the checking was wrong — too short, and the number of passing tests is not a check of the screen): several minutes on the running screen, without input and then with strokes; `goldfishTrack`'s `held`, `spent`, `crushed`, `shut` read over that time beside the captures; the same numbers from bubble/9 in the same window to compare.

Not known: 800 bubbles under 에이전트 2 (whether nearly the whole school is held, as in bubble/9); other presets; 전파; whether the threshold 0.5 is right; whether the bubble breathing in and out with no input reads well.

### The stroke

A finger drawn across the field lays a bubble every 30 px: one new object for the stroke (one not on the screen; in 전파 the object chosen there). Each comes in five times a fair share large (`STROKE_SIZE`) with appeal 2.5 (`STROKE_QUALITY`; 전파's newcomers had 1.5), so it is seen where it is drawn; the fish's own pull to new pages (bubble/9's) brings them. Before this it came in at nothing and was lost (artist: "인터랙션 자체가 전혀 안됨"). Both values are first guesses, looked at once.

### Performance (2026-11-25: not playable on a Windows computer, and glitches in the colours — both open)

The artist: the route is not playable on a Windows computer, and glitches keep showing in the colours. Neither was reproduced on the lead's machine (M4, 1209 × 639 CSS px, canvas 2418 px wide), and nothing was measured on the Windows one.

Measured here with `?measure` (which waits for the GPU each frame, so its times are above a normal frame's):
- **The rules cost almost nothing.** Against bubble/9 in the same window: web 0.6 against 0.5 ms, emit 0.9 against 0.3 ms, layout 4.2 against 4.1 ms. The frame is the renderer's, as in bubble/9: GPU 13 ms as the page opens, 27–31 ms once the large objects have grown (bubble/9: 27 ms).
- **More 흐름 버블** (500–1,150 a frame against bubble/9's 110–130: every contact is a link), but the GPU time did not follow their number (2,456 drawn at 13 ms, 750 at 30 ms).
- **A coarser canvas halves the GPU time**: `?ratio=1` (a quarter of the pixels) 10 ms against 16–26 ms.
- Without `?measure` both routes hold 60 fps here when the machine is idle; under load the layout step alone went to 10–20 ms.

Added (`loop/frame-loop.ts`, `pace`): frames slower than 28 ms for 4 s take the canvas down to 0.7 of its pixel ratio, again after 12 s if still slow, never under 0.6 device px a CSS px; nothing is judged in the first 30 s (the bake); it does not come back up until the page is reloaded. Each step re-bakes the objects (they show as plain spheres for some seconds). `window.goldfishRenderRatio` is the ratio in use; `?ratio=1` sets the most it may be. Seen once on the lead's machine under load (2 → 1.4). Whether it makes the Windows machine playable is not known: if the layout step (CPU) is what is slow there, it will not.

The route opens on **one bubble** (artist, 2026-11-25, after 200 the same day; `state/opening.ts`; bubble/9 and every measurement above have 800): the rest come from strokes. Seen once in the browser: one object in the middle, and a stroke laid some forty bubbles of one new object, each very large (with so few bubbles a fair share is a large part of the screen, and the stroke's bubbles come in at five times that). ## What bubble/9's feeling is made of (the starting point for a next attempt)

From `web/model.ts`, `web/iteration.ts`, `simulation/emitter.ts` and the bubble/1–7 notes. An agent-based model for this body has to supply each of these from what a bubble or a goldfish does; M9 supplies only some.

| In bubble/9 | What it does for the picture | In M9 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One conserved weight, moved along relations as portions that melt from the source's shape into the target's | Sizes change only by something visibly arriving or leaving; the flow never stops; one page growing shrinks the rest | Missing: size is a count of fish, nothing travels between bubbles |
| Attention weights relax towards appeal; appeal grows with rank | The rich get richer: a few large, many small | A crowd's pull on a fish (weak) |
| Quality drifts at random (volatility 1.5) | Leaders keep changing with no input | Forbidden on purpose: nothing new without a stroke |
| Tiring above 80 times a share (bubble/6) | No object keeps the screen; what swelled bursts | A burst at a capacity tuned for another scale |
| New pages, 0.85 a second, linking by appeal | Fresh material to move between | A stroke only |
| The school's watching raises a page's appeal; a watched page moves on to another object | The busy objects are the liquid ones | The reverse: the bubble *losing* fish changes |
| Links reach anywhere; position never feeds back | The inside of the raft lives | Neighbours are contacts, and fish cannot get inside |
