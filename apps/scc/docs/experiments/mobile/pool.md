# 포켓볼

`/mobile/pool/1` (2026-11-16) makes the mobile screen itself a pool table in three.js: the balls are the chief executives of US big tech, the cue ball is Trump, and the cloth is printed with dollar signs. It is at [`/mobile/pool/[experiment]`](../../../app/mobile/pool/[experiment]/page.tsx). Experimental.

- Tested relation: the screen is the table, not a picture of one. The table's outer edge is the screen's edge and the camera looks straight down over its middle (the long side fills 42° of view), so the rails frame the screen and the cloth fills it.
- Reference: [Evelyn Verdín's overhead photograph of a bar table](https://unsplash.com/photos/pool-table-ready-for-a-game-of-billiards-H6aZvgIaA6k) (Unsplash+, looked at, not copied or stored), read on 2026-11-16. Taken from it: dark wood rails with small inlaid sights, black moulded pocket castings, one strong lamp with the room dark around it, cushions shading the cloth beside them, and cues lying on the table before play. [Pure Pool](https://www.thexboxhub.com/new-shiny-xbox-one-pure-pool-screenshots-surface/) was looked at for ball gloss; it has no overhead view.
- Size: metres throughout. A 6 ft table (0.813 m between cushion noses, 128 mm from nose to outer edge). The balls are 1.55 × regulation (89 mm), enlarged twice at the user's request so the faces read: about 32 px on a 390 px screen; ball size need not be realistic. Pockets, cushion height, rail height and the cue's thickness are scaled with them. The playing length follows the screen (390 × 844 gives 0.81 × 2.06 m, longer than the real 1 : 2).
- Table (`scene/table.ts`): cloth bed with six real holes; six cushion strips as wedges under the same cloth (nose 1.05–1.31 ball radii high, 56 mm deep, ends cut back 52° at the corners and 14° at the sides); four rail boards mitred at the corners, each with its grain along its length, eighteen sights; a black casting 17 mm thick behind each pocket, 3 mm proud of the wood; each hole a tube 208 mm deep whose wall darkens with depth. Corner mouths are two balls wide, side mouths 2.2.
- Balls (`scene/textures.ts`): the pool set keeps its colours (solids 1–7 yellow, blue, red, purple, orange, green, maroon; black 8; stripes 9–15 with a 1¼ in band; ivory cue ball), and where a ball carries its number, twice on opposite sides, it carries a face instead: a round portrait 54° of the sphere in radius inside an ivory rim, laid on by angle from its centre so it is not stretched. Every ball is set down face up and the right way round. Racked as eight-ball, the 8 in the middle of the third row.
- Who (checked 2026-11-16 against each company's Wikipedia key-people entry; Huang against [Nvidia's 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm)): cue ball Donald Trump; 1 John Ternus (Apple, CEO since 2026-09-01), 2 Satya Nadella (Microsoft), 3 Sundar Pichai (Alphabet), 4 Andy Jassy (Amazon), 5 Mark Zuckerberg (Meta), 6 Elon Musk (Tesla), 7 Hock Tan (Broadcom), 8 Jensen Huang (Nvidia; resigned 2026-11-12, included at the user's request, on the black ball), 9 Lisa Su (AMD), 10 Lip-Bu Tan (Intel), 11 Sanjay Mehrotra (Micron), 12 Arvind Krishna (IBM), 13 Sam Altman (OpenAI), 14 Alex Karp (Palantir), 15 Marc Benioff (Salesforce). The numbering is this list's order, not a ranking. Left out: Oracle (two co-CEOs) and Netflix (not checked).
- Cloth: printed edge to edge with dollar signs in dark banknote green, two to a 170 mm tile, set like bricks, in the handset's bold serif (Georgia where present); cushions wear the same print.
- Light and material (`scene/stage.ts`): one spot lamp 1.5 m up and off to one side, 2048 px shadow map; reflections from a photographed billiard hall. Rails are walnut veneer (diffuse and normal map) stained dark under a clear coat. Cloth tooth is 1 mm noise used as relief under the print. A soft dark disc under each ball stands in for the lamp's width. ACES tone mapping.
- Assets (`public/3d/pool/`, 0.8 MB in all; sources and licences in its `license.txt`): `billiard_hall_512.hdr` (0.52 MB, Poly Haven CC0, the 1k panorama halved), `walnut_diff.jpg` and `walnut_nor.jpg` (Poly Haven CC0, 512 px, 49 and 20 kB), `faces.jpg` (0.21 MB: a 4 × 4 atlas of 256 px face crops from Wikimedia Commons portraits, public domain to CC BY-SA 4.0, in ball order). The user's limit for this folder is 5 MB.
- Physics (`model/physics.ts`, pure, 480 steps a second): cloth friction acts at the contact point, so a struck ball slides (μ 0.2) until its spin matches its speed, then rolls (μ 0.022); orientation is integrated from that spin. Ball collisions exchange only the velocity along the line of centres (restitution 0.95) and leave spin alone, so a sliding cue ball stops dead and a rolling one follows through. Cushions are line segments with their facings; rubber returns 0.8, less when hit hard, and the ball leaves a cushion already rolling along its rebound. A ball whose centre crosses a hole's edge falls under gravity inside the pocket's wall and is gone 208 mm down. Top speed 6.5 m/s. Not modelled: side spin, cue elevation, throw between balls, jumps.
- Cue and shot (`scene/game.ts`): the cue (tip, ferrule, maple shaft, joint, dark butt, bumper) lies on the table whenever a shot can be played, its tip 14 mm behind the cue ball, pointed at the nearest ball. Press anywhere and pull back: the cue turns to the pull and draws back up to 200 mm, and a hairline on the cloth runs to a ring where the cue ball will first touch. Letting go drives the cue through in 75 ms; a pull under 10 px leaves it lying. While balls roll the cue is drawn back off the table along its line, and it returns when they rest. Full power is a pull of 42 % of the short side.
- Break: ball contacts are resolved pair by pair, so a tight rack hit near its middle can pass most of the blow down one line of balls and leave the rest close together; a slightly off-centre break scatters it.
- Play: no turns or fouls. A pocketed cue ball returns to the head spot once everything rests; when the last object ball drops, the fifteen are racked again.
- Cost: frames are drawn only while a finger is down, during a stroke, while balls move or while the cue travels; the shadow map is redrawn on those frames. About 45 draw calls. Drawn pixels are capped at 2.6 million (pixel ratio at most 3), MSAA only below a pixel ratio of 2.5.

History (all 2026-11-16): (1) a flat black-and-white 2D canvas, rejected as not properly made. (2) A first three.js table built without references: 7 ft proportions (balls 19 px), flat satin-black rails, plain round holes, a generated three-lamp room for reflections, an even green plane, the cue appearing only during a pull; rejected as unrealistic and cheap, with odd physics. In that version a cushion halved a ball's spin without turning it, so a rolling ball lost about half its speed at every rail. (3) This version, from the reference above, first with regulation balls on the 6 ft table (22 px) and numbers on the balls; enlarged 1.3 ×, then 1.55 ×, at the user's request. (4) The same table given its subject at the user's request: faces for numbers, Trump as the cue ball, dollar signs across the cloth.

Checks (2026-11-16): lint and the navigation registry test pass; typecheck passes for these files (one unrelated error elsewhere in the app at the time). A 600-shot random simulation of the model left no overlapping, escaped or non-finite balls; every shot rested within 7.6 s (mean 3.6 s) and one 60 Hz frame of physics took 0.007 ms. The running route was opened in headless Chrome (Apple M4, Metal) at 390 × 844 @3× and looked at: the rack with every face up and the cue lying ready, the dollar print, corner and side pockets, a pull with the sighting line, a break in motion, and the table at rest with the cue re-addressed. Over a full-power break there, 380 frames ran at 16.67 ms mean, 16.8 ms max. Not yet played or timed on a mobile: touch feel, how the physics now reads in the hand, frame rate on a handset GPU and the corner pockets under rounded screen corners are unverified.
