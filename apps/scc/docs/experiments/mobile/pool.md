# 포켓볼

`/mobile/pool/1` (2026-11-16) makes the mobile screen itself a pool table in three.js: the balls are the chief executives of US big tech (face on one half, company mark on the other), the cue ball is Trump, the cloth is printed with dollar signs, and every ball that drops is told "You're fired". It is at [`/mobile/pool/[experiment]`](../../../app/mobile/pool/[experiment]/page.tsx). Experimental.

- Tested relation: the screen is the table, not a picture of one. The table's outer edge is the screen's edge and the camera looks straight down over its middle (the long side fills 42° of view), so the rails frame the screen and the cloth fills it.
- Reference: [Evelyn Verdín's overhead photograph of a bar table](https://unsplash.com/photos/pool-table-ready-for-a-game-of-billiards-H6aZvgIaA6k) (Unsplash+, looked at, not copied or stored), read on 2026-11-16. Taken from it: dark wood rails with small inlaid sights, black moulded pocket castings, one strong lamp with the room dark around it, cushions shading the cloth beside them, and cues lying on the table before play. [Pure Pool](https://www.thexboxhub.com/new-shiny-xbox-one-pure-pool-screenshots-surface/) was looked at for ball gloss; it has no overhead view.
- Size: metres throughout. A 6 ft table (0.813 m between cushion noses, 128 mm from nose to outer edge). The balls are 1.55 × regulation (89 mm), enlarged twice at the user's request so the faces read: about 32 px on a 390 px screen; ball size need not be realistic. Pockets, cushion height, rail height and the cue's thickness are scaled with them. The playing length follows the screen (390 × 844 gives 0.81 × 2.06 m, longer than the real 1 : 2).
- Table (`scene/table.ts`): cloth bed with six real holes; six cushion strips as wedges under the same cloth (nose 1.05–1.31 ball radii high, 56 mm deep, ends cut back 52° at the corners and 14° at the sides); four rail boards mitred at the corners, each with its grain along its length, eighteen sights; a black casting 17 mm thick behind each pocket, 3 mm proud of the wood; each hole a tube 208 mm deep whose wall darkens with depth. Corner mouths are two balls wide, side mouths 2.2.
- Balls (`scene/textures.ts`): each ball is two halves and nothing else: no ball colour, stripe, number or rim. One half is the chief executive's face, the other the company's mark on ivory; the cue ball is Trump's face with a bare ivory back. Either picture is wrapped over its half of the sphere by angle from its middle, so it keeps its proportions round to the side. Every ball is set down face up and the right way round, and shows its other half as it rolls. Racked in the eight-ball arrangement, Huang in the middle of the third row.
- Who (checked 2026-11-16 against each company's Wikipedia key-people entry; Huang against [Nvidia's 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm)): cue ball Donald Trump; 1 John Ternus (Apple, CEO since 2026-09-01), 2 Satya Nadella (Microsoft), 3 Sundar Pichai (Alphabet), 4 Andy Jassy (Amazon), 5 Mark Zuckerberg (Meta), 6 Elon Musk (Tesla), 7 Hock Tan (Broadcom), 8 Jensen Huang (Nvidia; resigned 2026-11-12, included at the user's request), 9 Lisa Su (AMD), 10 Lip-Bu Tan (Intel), 11 Sanjay Mehrotra (Micron), 12 Arvind Krishna (IBM), 13 Sam Altman (OpenAI), 14 Alex Karp (Palantir), 15 Marc Benioff (Salesforce). The numbering is only this list's order and the order of the atlases; nothing on a ball shows it. Left out: Oracle (two co-CEOs) and Netflix (not checked).
- Sound (`scene/sound.ts`): real recordings from a CC0 pack (cue strike, ball on ball, ball on cushion, a ball dropping into a pocket, a ball rolling), in one 50 kB sprite. Each impact the model reports plays a random take of its kind, louder and a little higher the faster it was, at most six starting in one frame; a ball rattling off a pocket's casting sounds as a cushion. The rolling recording loops under a low-pass, its level following the balls' summed speed. Each ball that goes down, the cue ball included, then plays Trump saying "You're fired" (0.92 s, from the White House's own video of his 2020-08-03 remarks), staggered 0.18 s when several drop together. Everything goes through a compressor so a break does not clip. The first cut (2026-11-16) was wrong: transcribed afterwards it said "workers than you will". The current cut transcribes as "You're fired." (Whisper large-v3-turbo) and has not been listened to. iOS Safari lets a page sound only after a tap, never after a touch that moved (WebKit bug 248265), and every shot is a drag: the user heard nothing on a mobile through two attempts that opened sound on press, then on release. So the page opens with the prompt the other mobile experiments use (a white card on the dimmed table: 소리를 켜고 시작합니다 / 시작); its tap opens the sound and the card goes once sound is out. A first try (2026-11-17) instead held the first shot drawn back until a tap: on a mobile that read as aiming that did nothing and a game that had become slow, and it was taken out the same day; shots fire on release again. The page asks iOS for the `playback` audio session, so the ringer switch does not mute it.
- Cloth: printed edge to edge with dollar signs in dark banknote green, two to a 170 mm tile, set like bricks, in the handset's bold serif (Georgia where present); cushions wear the same print.
- Light and material (`scene/stage.ts`): one spot lamp 1.5 m up and off to one side, 2048 px shadow map; reflections from a photographed billiard hall. Rails are walnut veneer (diffuse and normal map) stained dark under a clear coat. Cloth tooth is 1 mm noise used as relief under the print. A soft dark disc under each ball stands in for the lamp's width. ACES tone mapping.
- Assets (`public/3d/pool/`, 0.45 MB in all; sources and licences in its `license.txt`): `billiard_hall_256.hdr` (0.13 MB, Poly Haven CC0, the 1k panorama quartered), `walnut_diff.jpg` and `walnut_nor.jpg` (Poly Haven CC0, 512 px, 49 and 20 kB), `faces-768.jpg` (0.14 MB: a 4 × 4 atlas of 192 px face crops, scaled to 256 px cells on load from Wikimedia Commons portraits, public domain to CC BY-SA 4.0, in ball order), `logos-768.png` (0.04 MB, 96 colours: the same atlas layout of company marks from Commons SVGs, all trademarks; each fills its cell's circle, the symbol alone for Meta, Broadcom, AMD and Palantir, thin wordmarks stacked two or three deep, after the first, small atlas was hard to see on a ball), `youre-fired.mp3` (8 kB), `table.mp3` (50 kB, the table's sounds). Reduced on 2026-11-17 from 1.03 MB at the user's request. The user's limit for this folder is 5 MB.
- Physics (`model/physics.ts`, pure): each frame is cut into as many equal steps as the fastest ball needs to move no more than a fifth of its radius per step: two for a table nearly at rest, up to twenty-four for a break. Cloth friction acts at the contact point, so a struck ball slides (μ 0.2) until its spin matches its speed, then rolls (μ 0.022); orientation is integrated from that spin. Ball contacts are solved in up to four passes a step, skipping pairs at rest, exchanging only the velocity along the line of centres (restitution 0.95) and leaving spin alone, so a sliding cue ball stops dead and a rolling one follows through. The rack is set a fraction of a millimetre loose, as real racks are, which is what lets a square break scatter it. Cushions are line segments with their facings; rubber returns 0.8, less when hit hard, and the ball leaves a cushion already rolling along its rebound. A ball whose centre crosses a hole's edge falls under gravity and never goes into the table: toward the room it rolls over the cloth's edge as it sinks, toward the rail it is knocked back by the casting's wall, and below the cloth it stays inside the pocket's tube; it is gone 248 mm down. (Until 2026-11-17 a falling ball could sink half into the cloth and the casting; over 400 simulated shots that is now at most 1.8 mm in one frame.) Every ball, cushion and pocket impact is reported with its speed for the sound. Top speed 6.5 m/s. Not modelled: side spin, cue elevation, throw between balls, jumps.
- Cue and shot (`scene/game.ts`): the cue (tip, ferrule, maple shaft, joint, dark butt, bumper) lies on the table whenever a shot can be played, its tip 14 mm behind the cue ball, pointed at the nearest ball, its butt raised (4° up to 40°) just enough to pass over any ball and the rail under it rather than through them. Press anywhere and pull back: the cue turns to the pull and draws back up to 200 mm, and a hairline on the cloth runs to a ring where the cue ball will first touch. Letting go drives the cue through in 75 ms; a pull under 10 px leaves it lying. While balls roll the cue is drawn back off the table along its line, and it returns when they rest. Full power is a pull of 42 % of the short side.
- Play: no turns or fouls. A pocketed cue ball returns to the head spot once everything rests; when the last object ball drops, the fifteen are racked again.
- Cost: frames are drawn only while a finger is down, during a stroke, while balls move or while the cue travels; the shadow map is redrawn on those frames. About 45 draw calls. Drawn pixels are capped at 2.6 million (pixel ratio at most 3), MSAA only below a pixel ratio of 2.5.

History (all 2026-11-16): (1) a flat black-and-white 2D canvas, rejected as not properly made. (2) A first three.js table built without references: 7 ft proportions (balls 19 px), flat satin-black rails, plain round holes, a generated three-lamp room for reflections, an even green plane, the cue appearing only during a pull; rejected as unrealistic and cheap, with odd physics. In that version a cushion halved a ball's spin without turning it, so a rolling ball lost about half its speed at every rail. (3) This version, from the reference above, first with regulation balls on the 6 ft table (22 px) and numbers on the balls; enlarged 1.3 ×, then 1.55 ×, at the user's request. (4) The same table given its subject at the user's request: faces for numbers, Trump as the cue ball, dollar signs across the cloth. (5) Faces first sat in the number discs of coloured and striped balls; the user wanted the whole ball filled, then a face on one half and the company's mark on the other, and the "You're fired" recording on every drop. The physics step was changed from a fixed 480 Hz to as many steps as the frame needs, with a multi-pass contact solver and a slightly loose rack: before that a square break could pass down one line of balls and leave the rack standing.

Checks (2026-11-16): lint and the navigation registry test pass; typecheck passes for these files (one unrelated error elsewhere in the app at the time). A 600-shot random simulation of the model left no overlapping, escaped or non-finite balls; every shot rested within 7.8 s (mean 3.7 s) and one 60 Hz frame of physics averaged 0.004 ms (0.007 ms at the fixed 480 Hz step). Full-power breaks aimed square and up to 6 % off moved 10 to 15 of the fifteen balls more than 15 cm. The running route was opened in headless Chrome (Apple M4, Metal) at 390 × 844 @3× and looked at: the rack with every face up and the cue lying ready, balls at rest showing faces and marks, the dollar print, corner and side pockets, a pull with the sighting line, a break in motion, and the table at rest with the cue re-addressed. Over a full-power break there, 380 frames ran at 16.67 ms mean, 16.8 ms max. With the browser's normal autoplay rule and real touch events, the audio context was running after the first shot and the recording was started once per dropped ball (six over four shots), with signal measured at the output (peak 0.79 for one). It has not been listened to, so where the cut falls on the words is unverified; iOS and the ringer switch are untested. Not yet played or timed on a mobile: touch feel, how the physics now reads in the hand, frame rate on a handset GPU and the corner pockets under rounded screen corners are unverified.

## pool/2: the table tipped (2026-11-23)

`/mobile/pool/2` is `/1`'s table with nothing to strike and nowhere to fall. Experimental; its own copy of `/1`'s files (`components/mobile/pool/2`), sharing only the assets in `public/3d/pool/`.

- Tested relation: the table is the mobile: a ball on the screen is given exactly what the accelerometer says it would feel. Baseline `/1`; changed: the input (the mobile's own motion for the cue), gravity (a fifth), the pockets and cushions (none), the ball size, the opening prompt (none). Kept: the table's outer size, faces and marks, cloth print, wood, sights, lamp, camera, the rack as the starting position, the ball and cushion recordings.
- Table (`scene/table.ts`): the cloth lies sunk 97 mm between four plain mitred rail boards (68 mm wide, the same eighteen sights) and runs to the wood on every side: 0.93 m across. No cushions, holes, castings or cue. Balls are 2.2 × regulation (126 mm, about 46 px on a 390 px screen); the rack's middle sits a quarter of the way down the table.
- Input (`scene/game.ts`): `devicemotion`'s `accelerationIncludingGravity` is what a ball lying on the screen feels: gravity down whichever way the mobile is tipped, plus the mobile's own jolts, so a shake throws the balls and a mobile held up at 40° has them lying along its bottom rail. Its x and y go to the balls along the cloth and its z presses them on it, all scaled to the table's gravity, with 40 ms of smoothing, turned by `screen.orientation.angle` and by the quarter turn a wide viewport gives the table. iOS and Android report that vector with opposite signs; which one this is, is read off the first few readings against `deviceorientation`'s gravity (`cosβ·sinγ`, `−sinβ`, `−cosβ·cosγ`), whose axes are the same everywhere, and until then the tilt alone plays. Both sensors are listened to from the start; on iOS, which hands them over only inside a gesture, the first tap anywhere asks, and the same gestures open the sound. There is no card or button: until that tap an iOS table is still and silent. Before any sensor reading (a desktop, or iOS before the tap) a held finger or mouse tips the table toward itself, up to 30°; once a sensor has spoken the finger does nothing.
- Physics (`model/physics.ts`, pure): gravity is a fifth of the real thing and every force scales with it (the slope's pull, the cloth's grip μ 0.2, its rolling resistance μ 0.022), so the balls move as real ones would, played back 2.2 times slower; nothing else is tuned. `/1`'s sliding-and-rolling ball with the slope's pull added at its centre: a rolling ball keeps 5/7 of it, a resting ball starts above 1.8° of slope, and a slope too slight to start one still bends the path of one already rolling. Friction is in proportion to how hard gravity presses the ball on the cloth, so on a table stood on its edge the balls fall without turning. The rails are four straight walls returning 0.55. Contacts are solved in eight passes, balls and rails together. A closing speed no greater than what the slope alone adds in a step counts as leaning, not striking: no bounce, no sound, and a leaning ball turns only as far as it really travelled, so a heap along a rail lies still and silent.
- Sound (`scene/sound.ts`): `/1`'s sprite without the cue and pocket clips and without "You're fired".
- Cost: the model runs every frame a sensor speaks, but a frame is drawn only when some ball has moved more than 0.02 mm in a step: a heap lying on a tipped table, or a level table at rest, draws nothing. Against `/1`: drawn pixels capped at 1.9 million (2.6), shadow map 1024 px (1536), ball spheres 32 × 20 segments (40 × 26).

History (2026-11-23): (1) The first cut kept `/1`'s cushions as four cloth strips, took `deviceorientation`'s tilt at full gravity, and had `/1`'s 89 mm balls. On a mobile the user found it too sensitive, too fast and not realistic, read the cushion strips as a second, green margin inside the brown one, and asked for the green to be sunk and the balls larger. That cut also waited for a tap wherever `requestPermission` exists, which current Chrome has too. (2) The second cut took the pose the mobile was first read in as level (re-levelling over 8 s), gave the table 0.3 of the tilt from there, capped at 1.6 m/s², with 0.18 s of lag, while friction stayed at full gravity. The user: the physics is still not natural, and make it cheaper to run. The hidden level pulled balls with no tilt to explain it, the pull and the friction were on different scales (nothing moved under about 6°, and balls stopped short), and shaking did nothing. (3) This cut: the accelerometer as it is, one gravity for every force, no level, no cap, and the drawing costs above.

Checks (2026-11-23): lint and typecheck pass for these files; the registry test fails on another entry (`mobile/suneung/2`). Model simulation, 400 random slopes of 0–90° held 1.5 s each, every seventh at twice gravity, on a 0.93 × 2.17 m table: no ball left the cloth, none non-finite, deepest overlap in any frame 1.8 mm, 0.13 ms per 60 Hz frame. From the rack, the cue ball reaches the rail half a metre below it in 3.5 s at 5°, 1.7 s at 15°, 1.0 s at 40°, 0.7 s at 90°, and does not start at 1°; no impact is reported from a heap that has settled. The running route in headless Chrome (Apple M4, Metal) at 390 × 844 @3×, looked at, with synthetic `devicemotion` and `deviceorientation` in both the Android and the iOS sign convention, same result in each: no button or text; flat, nothing moves and nothing is drawn (0 draw calls in 2 s); held up at 40° the balls fall to the bottom rail and, once heaped, 0 draw calls in 2 s; tipped right they roll right (about 57 draw calls a frame while rolling, shadow pass included, 16.8 ms at the 95th percentile); lying flat and jerked right at 6 m/s² for 0.4 s, they are left behind to the left. The canvas is 936 × 2027 (1097 × 2374 before). Not verified: any real handset: whether it now reads as natural in the hand, the real sensors' signs and the landscape turn, the iOS permission tap, frame rate on a handset GPU, and the sound (not listened to). The finger path was not rerun in this cut.
