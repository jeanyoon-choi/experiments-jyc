# clock/1 — turning the clock's own cube

Experimental. Created 2026-10-20. Screen only: `/screen/clock/1` (no phone
route, no socket). Checked in desktop Chrome on the M4 (1470 × 703 CSS px, DPR 2).

## Tested relation

Source: the 1016 note "Moving-Image Cube": a moving image is an (x, y, t)
cube, and the screen is its xy section advancing along t. Option B turns that
section. [moving-image/1](../moving-image/1.md) applies Option B to recorded
footage (after Fels, Lee & Mase,
[Interactive Video Cubism](https://hci.rwth-aachen.de/publications/fels2000a.pdf), 2000).

The user's point: a moving image records reality and then fills the cube from
those frames. clock/1 puts the instrument that *measures* t in its place, with
nothing recorded. The cube is the clock itself, defined continuously in
(x, y, t). The face does not depend on t, so it is a cylinder. Each hand is a
helical sheet whose pitch is its period. moving-image/1's operation is
unchanged: a flat cut plane (quaternion), seen head-on, turned through the
block about either pivot. The question: what does the clock tell when its time
axis has been turned into space?

Author panel (bottom-right, open by default; copied from moving-image/2):
pitch and yaw sliders, pivot, span, clock speed (정지 / 실시간 / 60배 / 720배),

Arrow keys turn it 5°, and `0`/Home faces front.

## Structure

- `components/clock/1/model/` — pure math with `node:test` tests
  (`node --test components/clock/1/model/index.test.ts`). It covers the
  quaternions copied from moving-image/1's `plane.mjs`, the plane from the two
  angles, the box, the pivots (`timeFrame`), hand turns, digits per unit, and
  segment lit fractions (the shader uses the same rule). Tests check
  conventional hand angles, digit decoding, time-averaged segments, that an
  unturned plane shows exactly now in both pivots and all spans, that a 90°
  turn lays exactly one span across the width, the Fels sweep per span, and
  the sign convention.
- `components/clock/1/render/` — `shaders.ts` (slice and solid passes),
  `renderer.ts`, `use-clock-canvas.ts` (loop, easing, real-time clock).
- `components/clock/1/screen/` — the screen and the copied control panel.

## Observed (desktop Chrome, 2026-10-20, ~14:11–14:16 local)

Screenshots are in [`1/`](1/). Files marked `-mirrored` were taken before the
yaw sign was flipped: in them, later is to the left.

- **Flat, both stimuli:** a correct, normal clock at the browser's local time.
  At 14:15:01 the hour hand was a quarter past 2, the minute hand on 3 and the
  second hand just past 12. The digital row read 14:13:54 at the moment it was
  checked ([analogue-flat](1/analogue-flat.jpg), [digital-flat](1/digital-flat.jpg)).
- **Analogue, 1 min, yaw 20° (21 s on screen):** the second hand bends into an
  arc. Its root shows one second and its tip, further right, a later one, so a
  single hand points at two times. The face becomes an ellipse. The minute and
  hour hands stay straight
  ([analogue-1min-yaw20](1/analogue-1min-yaw20.jpg)).
- **Analogue, 1 h, yaw 20°–45° (17–42 min on screen):** the minute hand
  becomes a curved blade. The second hand is no longer a hand: it splits into
  20–40 faint near-vertical threads, one for each minute the screen spans, and
  each thread is the one moment in that column when the second hand passed
  through. The hour hand alone stays a straight, readable hand. The more
  precise the hand, the sooner turning destroys it
  ([analogue-1h-yaw45-mirrored](1/analogue-1h-yaw45-mirrored.jpg)).
- **Analogue, 1 h, yaw 90° (the y–t section through the hub):** the clock
  turns into a timeline. The hub becomes a horizontal line, which is the time
  axis itself. The 12 and 6 ticks become bands along the top and bottom edges.
  The second hand leaves one rust tick per minute (above the axis at :00 s,
  below it at :30 s): 60 ticks, a ruler. The minute hand appears once as a
  spike: up where it passed 12 (14:00, left), down where it passes 6 (14:30,
  right). The hour hand never crosses 12 or 6 in this hour, so it is absent.
  The instrument no longer shows a time. It has become a graduated axis, and
  you read the time by position along it, the way you read a chart
  ([analogue-1h-yaw90](1/analogue-1h-yaw90.jpg)).
- **Analogue, 1 h, pitch 90° (x–t, 28 min on screen):** the same ruler
  standing upright: rust rungs every minute, the minute hand's spike to the
  right at 14:15, and the hour hand as a thin gold sliver hugging the axis
  ([analogue-1h-pitch90](1/analogue-1h-pitch90.jpg)).
- **Analogue, 12 h, yaw 45° / 90°:** at 45° the hour hand is the curved blade
  and the minute hand becomes about eight threads. The second hand averages
  into a haze below visibility. At 90° the screen is a 12-hour ruler: one
  minute-hand spike per hour and a single gold hour spike at 12:00
  ([analogue-12h-yaw45-mirrored](1/analogue-12h-yaw45-mirrored.jpg),
  [analogue-12h-yaw90](1/analogue-12h-yaw90.jpg)). The clock is periodic, so
  at 12 h the two ends of the section are the same picture.
- **Fels pivot, 1 min, yaw 30°:** the cut is offset along its normal, so the
  hub sits off-centre. The box's far end shows as a black strip: outside this
  minute there is no time. The section moves across the block once a minute
  ([analogue-cube-1min-yaw30](1/analogue-cube-1min-yaw30.jpg)).
- **Digital, 1 h, yaw 20° (20 min on screen, centre 14:13:59):** the row reads
  crisply as about `14:14:38`, a time it was not. Each digit comes from the
  moment its own column cuts: the hours from minutes ago, the minute ones digit
  from a minute ahead. The seconds tens digit is a clean but chimeric glyph,
  and the seconds ones digit is shredded into vertical bars of ten different
  numerals. The analogue clock smears, so its ambiguity shows. The digital
  clock lies legibly ([digital-1h-yaw20](1/digital-1h-yaw20.jpg)).
- **Digital, 12 h, pitch 30° yaw 45° (10.5 h on screen):** the hours read
  `12` at 14:14. The minutes are striped chimeras. Both seconds digits read as
  dim `88`, because the time average of every numeral is nearly the all-lit
  glyph ([digital-12h-pitch30-yaw45](1/digital-12h-pitch30-yaw45.jpg)).
- **Digital, 1 min, pitch 90° (x–t through the middle segments):** a piano
  roll. Hours and minutes are steady bars. The seconds ones digit's middle
  segment switches on and off by numeral (0, 1 and 7 have none). Time becomes
  a score ([digital-1min-pitch90](1/digital-1min-pitch90.jpg)).
- **Digital, now pivot, yaw 90°:** the screen is empty. The section through
  x = 0 falls in the gap between the two minute digits, so the digital clock
  has no cross-section there at all
  ([digital-1min-yaw90](1/digital-1min-yaw90.jpg)). The Fels pivot sweeps x
  and does cut through the digits.
- **Solid view:** with the 1 min span the second hand is a visible rust
  helicoid around the hub axis, and the hour and minute hands are nearly flat
  blades. With 1 h, the minute hand is half a turn of a helicoid and the second
  hand a rust haze. The ticks run as 12 straight lines through time.

Performance: rAF holds 60 fps at DPR 2 (canvas 2940 × 1406) with the solid
inset, for both stimuli at all three spans, turned to pitch 30° yaw 45°. This
was measured by counting animation frames, not with GPU timer queries.

are clean; 7 model tests pass.

## Revision 2026-10-20 — time has thickness

The user's verdict on the first version: turned, the clock was "discrete and
a mess"; their reason: time has thickness, so it must read as continuous. Two
visual patches were tried and rejected the same day (a per-hand smooth phase
field switched in by hand speed, then by tilt: it drew a 1 h cube's hour hand
as a ±50° blob, which no clock does in an hour). The rule now follows from
the cut itself:

- On a turned cut neighbouring screen points lie at different times. A point
  owns the time its neighbourhood spans: Δt = L·|∇ₛt| (L, screen units,
  slider "시간 두께 L", default 0.06; ∇ₛt the cut's time gradient).
- Each point shows the clock averaged over that Δt (exact box filter), so a
  hand appears as the wedge it really sweeps in Δt: 360°·Δt/period.
- Flat, ∇ₛt = 0, so Δt = 0: the ordinary clock, unchanged.
- One rule for every hand. 1 h cube, y 30°, L 0.06: Δt ≈ 1 min — the hour
  hand sweeps 0.5° (sharp, straight), the minute hand 6° (a slight fan), the
  second hand a full turn (its strokes merge into a continuous faint disc).
  At y 90° the minute hand's crossing is a spike with swept flanks; the hour
  hand is absent within that hour, as it should be. (Tested in
  `model/index.test.ts`.)

## Fractal clock option (2026-10-20)

"프랙탈 시계": SCC fractal/clock/1's tree, one level shallower (depth 3, 40
clocks; `model/fractal.ts`, tested): every hand carries a child clock at its
tip, radius × 0.6; children run at the parent's rate × 0.86 / 1 / 1.14 and are
offset by 0.191 / 0.463 / 0.787 of 12 h. SCC runs it 12× fast; here it is
synced to the cube's clock time (the root is the ordinary clock). The tree is
a function of (x, y, t): each point places every child at its own time, so a
turned or bent cut bends the whole chain of clocks. Pre-order with subtree
sizes lets a point skip subtrees that cannot reach it; the walk keeps one
centre per level. Moving clocks widen their marks by their travel in the
point's time window (time thickness), so the box filter stays exact.

Performance (measured, M4, 2708×1406): the fractal on the slice runs at
60 fps; the cube view's raymarch evaluating the tree at every step pulled the
page to ~8–14 fps, so the volume draws only the root clock's sheets while the
cut face draws the whole tree — 60 fps with the cube view on, flat, turned and
bent. The canvas resolution also follows the measured frame time (steps down
below ~52 fps, back up above ~66 fps, never below 0.4 of the device ratio).
Time thickness L now defaults to 0.

## Antialiasing: tent instead of box (2026-10-20)

The user saw moiré (clock/2, fractal, sphere: fine concentric rings beating).
Marks were box-filtered over the pixel's footprint; a box's frequency
response falls off only as 1/f, so periodic marks near the pixel rate (the
second hand's crossings, the fractal's rings) alias into beats. `band` and
`periodicBand` now use a tent (box ⊗ box, half-width = the footprint, response
1/f²), computed exactly as a difference of the tent's cumulative function (so
it holds for any footprint, however narrow); windows wider than three periods
return the band's mean. Remaining: the fractal's fast-moving child clocks
leave sparse dotted traces on large rings (their own motion is widened
isotropically, not along their path).

## Interface revision (2026-10-20)

The bottom-left inset cube view is gone. A round button bottom-left (the
panel trigger's style, a cube icon) pops the 3D cube view out to fill the
screen; pressing it again returns to the 2D slice. The fractal clock is the
default stimulus.

## Unresolved

- **Yaw sign:** clock/1 sends the screen's right toward later, so a turned
  clock reads as a timeline. moving-image/1 uses the opposite sign. Should the
  two agree?
- **Second hand:** keep the continuous sweep (as SCC clock/1), or try a ticking
  second hand? A ticking hand would be a third, half-discrete stimulus: its
  sheets would be stepped staircases.
- **Digital at 90° with the now pivot:** empty because x = 0 is a gap. Shift
  the row so that the centre falls on a digit, or keep the emptiness as a
  finding?
- **Timer accuracy:** fps was measured by rAF only; GPU frame time is not
  measured. A smaller window or a 1× DPR is untested.
- **Other proposal (not built):** a "photograph" stimulus, a still image
  extruded along t. Turning it would only stretch it. That is the null case,
  and it would show that every structure seen above comes from the clock's
  motion. It is listed here, not built.
- **Portrait viewports and phones:** not checked.
