# clock/1-3d — turning the cut through a real 3D clock

Experimental. Created 2026-10-20. Screen `/screen/clock/1-3d` (screen only).

## Tested relation

clock/1 turned the cut plane through the (x, y, t) cube of a flat clock.
Here the cube is the moving image of a real, three-dimensional clock: each
screen pixel is a cube point (x, y, t), (x, y) the point on the film of a
fixed perspective camera and t the scene time. The ray through (x, y) is
traced into the clock with every hand at its angle at time t, so a turned
cut shows one physical clock whose hands stand at different times across
the picture — with their real heights and highlights. Flat (unturned)
it is an ordinary still of the clock at the local time.

The first attempt (by a subagent, discarded the same day) copied clock/1's SCC
clock into 3D, then pre-rendered flat hand sprites rotated in 2D, on a wall
with numerals and a brand: rejected as cheesy, with surroundings, and not
three-dimensional at all.

## The clock

A minimal wall clock of the Max Bill / Junghans kind, in metres, as an
analytic SDF (`render/scene-glsl.ts`):

- Case: brushed aluminium cylinder, 300 mm across, 45 mm deep, one rounded
  edge; no bezel steps.
- Dial: matte off-white, printed hour batons (quarters heavier) and a minute
  track, antialiased over the pixel's footprint on the dial. No numerals, no
  text.
- Hands: satin-black hour and minute hands (tapered bars with rounded
  edges), red lacquered second hand with a round counterweight, stacked at
  2.8 / 4.2 / 5.7 mm above the dial on a small hub with a polished cap.
- Flat glass with a faint Fresnel reflection of the studio.
- No surroundings: the void #1c1d1b. Light: one key from the upper left, a
  dark studio environment (one large soft box, one strip) for reflections,
  GGX specular, ACES tone mapping. No cast shadows and no occlusion (removed
  at the user's request, 2026-10-20): only the surfaces' own shading.
- Camera: perspective, straight in front of the clock on its axis (an oblique view was tried first and rejected: the opening must be the clock seen head-on).

## Interface

clock/1's panel: x/y plane angles, pivots, spans (1 min / 1 h / 12 h), speeds,
face front, back to now; drag/keys turn; bottom-left button pops out the 3D
cube view (box, cut face rendered as the clock, edges).

## Observed (M4 Chrome, 2026-10-20)

- Flat: the clock at 15:36, the hands' form read from their own shading and
  the oblique view (no shadows).
- y 30°, 1 h: the hour and minute hands bend across the face; the second hand
  becomes thin red threads, one per minute.
- y 75°: the face stretches; the minute hand becomes a long curve.
- 2940×1726, 60 fps (also with the cube view).
- Bug found and fixed while building: thin hands stepped over by the march
  (bound-limited steps).

## Unresolved

- One ray per pixel: the second hand's threads in turned cuts alias slightly.
- The fractal tree of 3D clocks is not built yet.
