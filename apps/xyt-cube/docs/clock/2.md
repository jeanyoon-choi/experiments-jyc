# clock/2 — bending the clock's cube, keeping the plane

Experimental. Created 2026-10-20. Screen `/screen/clock/2` (screen only).

## Tested relation

clock/1 applied moving-image/1's operation (turn the cut plane) to a clock
defined in (x, y, t). clock/2 applies moving-image/2's operation: the screen
stays the ordinary flat present, and the clock's cube itself is bent. The
question: when time's own geometry is bent, what does the same flat present
show of a clock?

- The cube is the block of one span (1 min / 1 h / 12 h) containing now; the
  present sweeps it once per span (the clock's time scale replaces the clip's
  duration). Rate 60× / 720× makes the sweep visible.
- Geometries, copied from moving-image/2 (`model/geometry.mjs`): non-orthogonal
  axes (shear), cylindrical and spherical coordinates, constant curvature K,
  Clifford torus — and, instead of the Möbius flux (a conformal boost, not a
  Möbius band), a Möbius ring: the cube's t axis is bent into a circle about
  an axis parallel to y (at x = −0.5/a), and its (x, y) frame turns about t
  by φ = a·π·t/depth on the way round; at a = 1 the circle closes and the
  ends are glued with a half-turn, (x, y, t_max) ~ (−x, −y, t_min). The flat
  present cuts this ring obliquely, so across one screen both the time and
  the twist change, and the far side crosses the seam into the flipped other
  end as the present passes the ring's middle. (A first version only turned
  the frame rigidly by φ(t): on the fixed flat present that is one rotated
  clock, the same cut as before — rejected by the user.)
- Time has thickness (clock/1's rule, generalised to a curved cut): a point
  owns the time its neighbourhood of size L spans, Δt = L·|∇ₛt|, with ∇ₛt the
  cut's local time gradient from screen derivatives of the cube point. Each
  point shows the clock averaged over Δt and its pixel footprint (exact box
  filters). Flat, ∇ₛt = 0: the ordinary clock. A Möbius / loop seam is not a
  gradient; Δt is capped there.

Each geometry is the identity at amount 0, where the screen is the running
clock at the local time.

## Interface

Author panel (bottom-right, open): geometry, amount sliders, time thickness
L, cube depth (span), clock speed, analogue / digital, cube view, flatten,
back to now. Drag or arrow keys bend; `0` flattens. The bottom-left inset is
the bent block (past half solid, hands' sheets as a volume, the flat screen
rectangle as an outline); clicking it swaps it with the slice, clicking again
swaps back (also added to clock/1).

## Fractal clock option (2026-10-20)

"프랙탈 시계": SCC fractal/clock/1's tree, one level shallower (depth 3, 40
clocks; `model/fractal.ts`, tested): every hand carries a child clock at its
tip, radius × 0.6; children run at the parent's rate × 0.86 / 1 / 1.14 and are
offset by 0.191 / 0.463 / 0.787 of 12 h. SCC runs it 12× fast; here it is
synced to the cube's clock time (the root is the ordinary clock). The tree is
a function of (x, y, t): each point places every child at its own time, so a
turned or bent cut bends the whole chain of clocks. Pre-order with subtree
sizes lets a point skip subtrees that cannot reach it; the walk keeps one
centre per level. Moving clocks widen their marks by their travel in the
point's time window (time thickness), so the box filter stays exact.

Performance (measured, M4, 2708×1406): the fractal on the slice runs at
60 fps; the cube view's raymarch evaluating the tree at every step pulled the
page to ~8–14 fps, so the volume draws only the root clock's sheets while the
cut face draws the whole tree — 60 fps with the cube view on, flat, turned and
bent. The canvas resolution also follows the measured frame time (steps down
below ~52 fps, back up above ~66 fps, never below 0.4 of the device ratio).
Time thickness L now defaults to 0.

## Antialiasing: tent instead of box (2026-10-20)

The user saw moiré (clock/2, fractal, sphere: fine concentric rings beating).
Marks were box-filtered over the pixel's footprint; a box's frequency
response falls off only as 1/f, so periodic marks near the pixel rate (the
second hand's crossings, the fractal's rings) alias into beats. `band` and
`periodicBand` now use a tent (box ⊗ box, half-width = the footprint, response
1/f²), computed exactly as a difference of the tent's cumulative function (so
it holds for any footprint, however narrow); windows wider than three periods
return the band's mean. Remaining: the fractal's fast-moving child clocks
leave sparse dotted traces on large rings (their own motion is widened
isotropically, not along their path).

## Interface revision (2026-10-20)

The bottom-left inset cube view is gone. A round button bottom-left (the
panel trigger's style, a cube icon) pops the 3D cube view out to fill the
screen; pressing it again returns to the 2D slice. The fractal clock is the
default stimulus. clock/2 opens on 구면 좌표 at 1/반지름 = −1.82 (shell centre ahead of the front face).

## Unresolved

- Whether the block (span) is the right "cube" for a clock, whose time has no
  ends: the deformation is centred on the block, and the present jumps to the
  next block's back face once per span.
