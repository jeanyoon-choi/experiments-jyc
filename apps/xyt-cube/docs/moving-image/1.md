# moving-image/1 — rotating the projection plane (video cubism)

Experimental. Created 2026-10-20. Screen `/screen/moving-image/1`; phone
`/mobile/moving-image/1`. Real-phone tilt is **unverified**; the screen and the
phone's drag fallback were checked in desktop Chrome against the running app
(relay not yet restarted, so both ran on the local fallback).

## Tested relation

Source: the 1016 "xyt Moving Img Cube" note, Option B — keep the (x, y, t)
cube and change the 2D projection: rotate or tilt the projected plane, via the
phone's accelerometer. The direct precedent is Fels, Lee & Mase,
[Techniques for Interactive Video Cubism](https://hci.rwth-aachen.de/publications/fels2000a.pdf)
(ACM Multimedia 2000; earlier Fels & Mase, NPIVM 1999): frames stacked as a
volume, normal playback is a cut plane parallel to xy advancing along t, and
the user rotates and moves the cut plane (trackball) to see other slices.

Here the clip is one 3D texture; the screen shows the cut plane head-on and
nothing else. The cube's geometry never changes, only the plane does.
Option A (changing the cube itself) is not part of this variant.

| Element | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Plane | a quaternion; identity = the xy plane = the ordinary video |
| Pivot `now` (default) | the plane turns about the centre of the present frame, which advances along t; time loops, so the screen centre is always "now" and a tilt reaches into past and future across the screen |
| Pivot `cube` (Fels) | the plane sweeps the box along its normal once per clip duration; outside the box is black, so tilted cuts are 3–6-sided polygons |
| Box | aspect × 1 × max(aspect, 1) frame heights (Fels: depth = width) |
| Phone | press and hold: the phone's rotation since the last reading is applied to the plane in the plane's own frame (phone screen = cut, top = up); released, the plane stays. Without motion, drag turns it |
| Many phones | each sends deltas; they compose in arrival order on one shared plane |
| Phone view | the box seen from a fixed three-quarter view, the half on the viewer's side of the plane removed: the cut face is the slice, the rest of the solid is the clip's x–t and y–t faces |

Screen (author) controls, bottom-right: clip, play/still/reverse, pivot,
optional cube inset, face front. Drag or arrow keys turn the plane on the
screen itself; `0`/Home faces front.

## Structure

- `socket/experiments/moving-image/1/` — `plane.mjs` (quaternions, device
  orientation, box, plane centre; shared with browsers), `model.mjs`
  (orientation, playhead, rate, clip, pivot), `index.mjs` (events
  `moving-image-1:*`, room `experiment:moving-image:1`), tests beside them.
- `components/moving-image/1/` — `media/` (clip registry, sheet → volume loader),
  `render/` (WebGL2 3D texture; `slice` and `solid` passes), `transport/`
  (sole socket boundary; runs the same model locally while the relay is
  unreachable), `screen/`, `mobile/`.
- Clips: `scripts/build-cube.sh <video> <id> [seconds] [start]` (`FPS`,
  `PREVIEW_H` env) writes `public/media/moving-image/<id>/`: `clip.mp4` at the
  source's own resolution (it refuses anything under 1920×1080; wider than
  2048 px is scaled to 1920 wide, the 3D texture limit) and small preview
  sheets for phones. The screen seeks through the mp4 once, frame by frame,
  and converts each frame on the GPU into a Y volume plus a half-size CbCr
  volume (the mp4's own 4:2:0, so no further loss); frames not yet read are
  black. Unturned, each point reads its nearest frame, so unturned
  playback shows the clip's own frames; where a turned plane runs across
  time, neighbouring frames blend (weighted by frames crossed per pixel), so
  the slice has no steps. Cost: 1.5 B/px, 3.1 MB per 1920×1080 frame; keep about 300 frames
  (≈0.9 GB). The screen renders at up to 2× device pixels.
- Registered (all Blender Foundation open movies, CC BY 3.0, 1920×1080,
  three kinds of footage):
  - `sintel-trailer` — [Sintel](https://durian.blender.org/) trailer, 12–42 s,
    10 fps, 300 frames, 21 MB: dark fantasy animation, montage of short shots
    (letterbox baked into the source).
  - `bbb-trailer` — [Big Buck Bunny](https://peach.blender.org/) trailer,
    4–30 s, 10 fps, 260 frames, 20 MB: bright cartoon, shots between white title cards.
  - `tears-of-steel` — [Tears of Steel](https://mango.blender.org/) film,
    262–274.5 s at 24 fps (300 frames): one continuous take (no cuts), a
    tracking walk along an Amsterdam canal — the stable, single-scene clip
    against the two montages, built at full frame rate so slices across a
    moving figure stay fine (at 10 fps they comb). The master is 1920×800 scope; `LETTERBOX=1`
    pads it to 1920×1080.
  Earlier clips (Gen-2 1408×768, a 360×640 phone clip, a 960×540 synthetic
  loop, and the author's own films) were removed: none meets 1920×1080, and
  the author's own work is not to be used.

## Invariants

- The relay owns the plane, playhead, rate, clip and pivot; browsers derive
  centre, slices and the solid view. Phones send raw deltas; the display
  smooths orientation once.
- Unturned, the screen is the clip itself.
- Outside the frame's x, y is black.

## Observed (desktop Chrome, 2026-10-20)

- Identity: the GenAI clip plays normally.
- `cube` pivot, 30° about y while playing: most of the sweep shows only a
  narrow polygon, because the plane rotates about the box centre and grazes
  its corners. Legible as a section of a solid, weak as a screen. This led to
  the `now` pivot as default.
- `now` pivot, 30° about y: the frame stays filled; each column is a
  different moment; the loop seam is a visible vertical cut (the end of the
  clip meeting its start).
- 90° about y (still): the x–t slice — horizontal time trails of the static
  room (Fels' fig. 3). The GenAI clip is nearly static, so its slices are
  mostly streaks; the handheld cat clip bends more.
- Multi-shot clip (2026-10-20, now `sintel-trailer`): at a ~20° turn about y
  the screen becomes vertical bands, one shot per band — the montage laid out across
  space instead of time (the note's "Scene A | Scene B: 붙이면 Montage,
  가로질러 자르면 단면"). Shot cuts show as hard vertical edges. With a fixed
  box depth a 30 s clip is steep: small tilts already span most of it.
- 1920×1080 (2026-10-20, M4 Chrome): identity is the source frame, sharp.
  Tilted, x stays sharp but moving edges step in columns: at 10 fps each
  frame owns a strip of the slice. More fps means fewer seconds per GB.
  Seeking all 300 frames takes about 2 s.
- Phone view: the cut face reads as "now" on a solid of the past while
  playing; an oblique drag gives a diagonal cut through the solid.

## Unresolved

- Real-phone feel of the hold-to-turn mapping (sign, gimbal behaviour near an
  upright phone, iOS permission).
- The loop seam: keep it as evidence of the cut, or hide it with a crossfade
  or a ping-pong clip.
- Temporal resolution vs length at 1920×1080 (memory bound): 10 fps × 30 s
  now; 24 fps would fit ~12 s.
- Box depth for long clips: tie it to duration (gentler time per tilt) or
  keep it fixed (steeper, more shots at once)?
- Fels's cut sphere (a non-linear surface) is not implemented.
- Option A (geometry of the cube itself) remains open; spacetime/1 is a
  failed attempt at it (see its doc).
